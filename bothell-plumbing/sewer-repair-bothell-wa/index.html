    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Bothell

    When trenchless repair isn't possible due to severe damage or specific pipe configurations, traditional dig-and-replace methods are necessary.     Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Bothell, WA

    While often overlooked, the sewer vent pipe is essential for proper drainage and preventing sewer gas buildup. Issues with vent pipes, such as blockages from animal nests or damage from strong winds, can lead to slow drains, gurgling noises and foul odors. FHN Plumbing diagnoses and repairs vent pipe problems, ensuring your entire drainage system works correctly and safely and protecting good indoor air quality.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Bothell, WA

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water and solids. Bellies usually form where the original bedding washed out or where the soil settled after years of wet weather. Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. We confirm the depth of standing water on camera, then re-bed and re-grade that section to restore proper fall. Correct slope is typically a quarter inch per foot for a typical residential line.

    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Bothell, WA

    For many sewer line issues, especially those caused by tree roots or pipe corrosion, trenchless repair offers a less invasive option. This method, which includes pipe bursting or pipe lining, requires minimal excavation, saving your yard and landscaping from extensive disruption. We use specialized equipment to either pull a new pipe through the old one or insert a resin-coated liner that cures in place, creating a new, durable pipe inside the old one. It's an effective and popular choice for preserving property appearance in Bothell.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Bothell

    Some older homes still have Orangeburg pipe, a tar-paper material that flattens and blisters over time. Cast iron from the same era corrodes from the inside and scales up until little flow remains. When a camera shows widespread failure, patching is a waste of money. We replace the line in sections or end to end with PVC, and we can usually use trenchless methods to avoid tearing up the whole yard.
