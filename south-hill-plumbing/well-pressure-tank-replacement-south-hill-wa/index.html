    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in South Hill, WA

    Most modern well pressure tanks are bladder-style, separating air and water for better efficiency. When the bladder fails, you'll experience symptoms like short-cycling of the well pump. FHN Plumbing carefully replaces these bladder-style tanks across South Hill, WA, ensuring proper pre-charge pressure and system integration for long-lasting performance.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in South Hill, WA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.

    Preventative Well Pressure Tank Maintenance in South Hill, WA

    While our primary service is well pressure tank replacement, FHN Plumbing also emphasizes the importance of preventative maintenance for your well system in South Hill, WA. Regular checks of your pressure tank's air charge and overall condition can help identify potential issues before they lead to a complete failure. During a maintenance visit, we can top off the air charge, inspect for early signs of corrosion or leaks, and advise on steps to maximize the lifespan of your current tank, potentially delaying the need for replacement.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in South Hill, WA

    Diaphragm tanks like the Amtrol Well-X-Trol use a fixed membrane that separates air above from water below. They are durable, but the steel shell can rust through at the base after years of condensation in a hot, damp equipment space. We replace corroded diaphragm tanks and check the stand and fittings under them. The new tank is leveled and supported so it does not stress the piping. This restores even pressure to every faucet.

    Well-Rite Pressure Tank Installation in South Hill, WA

    Well-Rite pressure tanks are a popular choice known for their reliability and durable construction, featuring heavy-duty steel shells and high-quality butyl diaphragms. At FHN Plumbing, we frequently install Well-Rite tanks as part of our well pressure tank replacement services in South Hill. These tanks are designed to provide consistent water pressure and reduce pump cycling, extending the life of your well pump. We ensure proper sizing and installation of Well-Rite tanks, adhering to manufacturer specifications for full performance and warranty validity.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in South Hill

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and choke flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives accurate readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in reliability.

    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in South Hill

    When your well pressure tank shows signs of trouble, it is important to determine if a repair is feasible or if full replacement is necessary. Small air leaks or issues with the air valve might be repairable. However, if the tank itself is corroded, has a ruptured diaphragm, or is waterlogged, replacement is almost always the more economical and reliable option. Our technicians in South Hill provide an honest assessment, explaining the pros and cons of repair versus a complete well pressure tank replacement, so you can make an informed decision.

    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in South Hill

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the available water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the best ways to extend pump life.
