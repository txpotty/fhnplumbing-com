    PEX Pipe Replacement in Kennewick, WA

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Because PEX flexes, it absorbs some of the shock that causes water hammer, and long runs with few fittings mean fewer places for a leak to start inside a wall. We keep PEX out of sunlight and away from water heater flues.

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step.     Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Kennewick

    Most whole-home repipes in Kennewick, WA fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. The final number depends on square footage, how many fixtures need new lines, whether you choose PEX or copper and how much drywall has to be opened and patched along the route. The quote comes in writing.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper is durable but costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    Rusty or brown water, low pressure at several fixtures and repeated pinhole leaks are the classic signs. Noisy pipes can be another. If your home has galvanized steel, polybutylene or Kitec, a repipe is often worth planning before a failure forces the issue, since those materials tend to fail across the whole house rather than in one spot.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most Washington jurisdictions. We pull the permit and book the inspections.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Openings are usually small. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. Copper jobs often get Viega ProPress fittings.
