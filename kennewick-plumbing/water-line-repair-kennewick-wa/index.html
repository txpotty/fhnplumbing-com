    PEX Water Line Repair in Kennewick, WA

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Underground Leak Detection in Kennewick, WA

    A ground microphone picks up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe, even through a couple of feet of dirt. Quiet leaks are harder. For those we push a hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas into the drained line and sniff for it at the surface, which finds leaks the microphone cannot hear. Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. You get a written price as soon as the leak is marked on the ground.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Kennewick

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Kennewick

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Kennewick

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    Main Water Line Repair in Kennewick

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Kennewick

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. Then the new pipe gets pulled through underground. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Kennewick

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Full replacement costs more. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can top $5,000 when the run is long, deep or under hardscape, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Spot repairs are often exempt. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Copper is Type K for buried runs. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. We do not use cheap knockoff fittings.

    Most replacements get Type K copper or PEX. HDPE goes in on trenchless pulls. Copper lasts a very long time and resists punctures, and PEX flexes with the soil, tolerates freezing better and goes in faster. We recommend one based on your soil, line length, budget and what your local code allows in Kennewick.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.
