    Pressure Tanks for Well Houses and Barns in Kennewick, WA

    Tanks in outbuildings face heat, cold and damp. Moisture eats steel tanks from the bottom. We often recommend a composite tank in those spaces, raise it off the floor on a stand, and insulate exposed piping and the switch when the building sees freezing weather. Simple protection.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Kennewick

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. We shut off power to the pump first. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and plumb it back in with fresh thread sealant and new fittings. Usually a morning.

    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in Kennewick, WA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. It works with the tank, not apart from it. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, and a cheap new gauge beats guessing. Small parts, big role.

    Flex-Lite Pressure Tank Replacement in Kennewick

    Flex-Lite tanks have a composite shell. They will not rust. That makes them a good pick for damp basements, crawlspaces and well houses where steel tanks tend to corrode from the bottom up, and they are lighter to carry into tight spaces. Same plumbing as steel.

    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in Kennewick, WA

    A bladder tank holds water inside a rubber balloon surrounded by compressed air. Air pressure forces water to the fixtures. Bladders crack with age, especially if the tank ran with low pre-charge, which waterlogs the tank. Replacement is the fix.

    Galvanized Steel Pressure Tank Upgrades in Kennewick

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Air and water share the same space. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, which means draining it to restore the air. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.

    Same-Day Tank Swaps in Kennewick

    We keep common tank sizes on our trucks. That means a call in the morning can often end with a new tank installed by afternoon, including new tank tee, gauge and relief valve, because the parts are already with us instead of on a supply house shelf. Bigger tanks may take a day to order.

    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Kennewick, WA

    Constant pressure systems use a variable speed drive that adjusts pump speed to demand. You get even pressure across fixtures. These systems can run on a smaller tank, but they cost more up front and need a compatible pump and drive. We explain the trade-off.
