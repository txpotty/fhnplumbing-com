    Pressure Reducing Valve and Expansion Tank Pairing in Auburn

    Many homes see high incoming pressure, so pressure reducing valves are common. The PRV protects your fixtures but also turns your plumbing into a closed system, which makes an expansion tank necessary. We test the PRV output and replace it if it is creeping or failing. Pairing a healthy PRV with a correctly charged expansion tank gives you steady pressure throughout the house.

    Brands and Tank Materials in Auburn, WA

    We install potable-rated thermal expansion tanks from brands such as Watts, Amtrol and A.O. Smith. Potable tanks use a butyl diaphragm and a lined interior so water never touches bare steel. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is typically soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Stainless steel connections resist corrosion from hard or aggressive water. We avoid tanks designed only for hydronic heating, since they are not approved for drinking water.

    Replacing Old Expansion Tanks in Auburn

    Expansion tanks do not last forever, and an old, waterlogged or corroded tank offers no protection. If your existing tank in Auburn is showing signs of failure, such as a dripping relief valve or fluctuating water pressure, it is time for a new one. We carefully remove the old unit, dispose of it responsibly and install a new, correctly sized expansion tank. This proactive replacement prevents costly damage to your water heater and plumbing system and restores proper pressure control.

    Expansion Tank Replacement in Auburn, WA

    Expansion tanks wear out over time as the internal bladder or diaphragm fails and the tank fills with water. A waterlogged tank feels heavy, and tapping it produces a dull sound all the way up instead of a hollow top. Pressing the air valve and getting water instead of air is a sure sign it has failed. We replace failed tanks with a correctly sized new unit and check the precharge before installation.

    Expansion Tanks With Tankless Water Heaters in Auburn, WA

    Tankless water heaters hold very little water, so thermal expansion is minimal in most homes. However, homes with recirculation loops, storage buffer tanks or certain closed system setups may still benefit from an expansion tank. We evaluate your system and only recommend a tank when it serves a real purpose. This keeps you from paying for parts you do not need.
