    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Auburn

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, while new HDPE comes through right behind the head. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Auburn

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, usually in one working day from start to backfill.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Auburn

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. Confirming it takes about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Auburn

    Underground leaks hide well. The first clue is often a water bill that jumped for no reason. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Auburn, WA

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. FHN Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Auburn

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Auburn, WA

    Besides repairs, FHN Plumbing handles service line upgrades when a commercial tenant needs more flow, like a restaurant adding a dish line or a second restroom. Bigger meters need bigger pipe. We size it to the fixture load. Then we coordinate with the water provider on the tap and meter change, set the backflow assembly and get the inspection signed off, so the tenant is not held up waiting on paperwork.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Auburn

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Auburn

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    Most jurisdictions in Washington require a permit for a full service line replacement. Small repairs depend on the city. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, and an inspector looks at the new pipe, the bedding and the burial depth before backfill. FHN Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Glacial till and dense hardpan are common in western Washington, and they drain poorly enough to keep water against foundations and in crawlspaces. Tree roots wrap and crush shallow lines over time, while mineral scale builds up inside older pipe and thins the wall. Galvanized pipe fails most often.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, utility marking, careful excavation and burial-rated fittings. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Know where the pipe runs. Disconnecting hoses, insulating crawlspace pipes and protecting hose bibs before the first freeze prevents most winter damage. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and readings above 80 psi mean the line is under more stress than it should be. On galvanized lines, planning a replacement beats waiting for the break.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull can stretch to two or three days, mostly because of restoration work and inspection timing. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    Small spot repairs often do not need one. A new service line almost always does. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Auburn, so FHN Plumbing checks the local requirement for your address and pulls the permit when one applies. The inspector sees the trench before it is backfilled.
