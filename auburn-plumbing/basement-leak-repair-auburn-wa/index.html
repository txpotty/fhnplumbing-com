    Water Heater and Appliance Leak Repair in Auburn, WA

    Water heaters, softeners and washing machines often sit in basements, and their leaks pool on the floor out of sight. A tank leaking at the seam is done.     Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Auburn

    A sump pump sits in a pit below the slab and kicks on when a float rises. Most failures come from a stuck float switch, a burned-out motor, or a check valve that lets the discharge water fall back into the pit and short-cycles the pump. We test yours with a bucket. A battery backup is worth adding where power drops in storms.

    Crack Injection for Basements in Auburn

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. Ports go along the crack, the face gets sealed, and resin goes in from the bottom up until it fills the full depth. Epoxy adds strength. For a crack that is actively wet, polyurethane works better since it reacts with water.

    Emergency Basement Flooding Response in Auburn

    Sump Pump Repair and Installation in Auburn, WA

    Price depends on the source. Fixing a leaking fitting or supply line is usually a few hundred dollars. Crack injection and sump work cost more, and perimeter drains or exterior waterproofing can run several thousand. We quote after testing.

Coverage for seepage work depends on the method, since an interior drain system can carry a long warranty while a crack injection is warranted against leaking at that crack, and we write the terms out before you approve.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Wet cracks get polyurethane. Our interior drains use rigid perforated pipe, which keeps its slope and can be jetted later.

    Start outside. Clean gutters and push downspout discharge six feet or more from the wall. Make sure the soil slopes away from the house, test the sump pump each spring by filling the pit with a bucket of water, and insulate cold water lines that sweat in humid weather. Check the water heater yearly.

    A pipe or fitting repair usually takes an hour or two. Injecting a crack is a half-day job. Sumps take most of a day, and perimeter drains take two to five days depending on wall length.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Inside, pour a bucket of water into the sump pit every spring to make sure the pump kicks on, check the water heater and washer hoses each year, and replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones every five years or so.

    Only some repairs need excavation. Outside membranes and new footing drains require digging to the footing.
