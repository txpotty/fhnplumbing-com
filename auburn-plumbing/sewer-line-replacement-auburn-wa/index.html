    Sewer Line Replacement Methods in Auburn, WA

    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Auburn

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. When the camera stops at a pile of broken clay or a flattened section of Orangeburg, the only fix is to dig down, clear the debris and lay new pipe through that stretch. Digging also lets us see the soil around the break. Often there is a void or a root mat right at the failure point.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Auburn

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Where we can, the sod is cut and set aside before the digging starts. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.

    After-Hours Sewer Collapse Response in Auburn

    Raw sewage on a basement floor is a health problem. Our on-call tech can clear a partial blockage, scope the line and tell you if the house can safely use its drains overnight or if everything needs to stay off until the repair. Cleanup comes after the pipe is fixed. If drywall or carpet got wet, we can refer a restoration company.

    Emergency Sewer Collapse Repair in Auburn

    Every drain in the house stops when a lateral collapses. The tech scopes the line, marks the break and lets you know whether the dig can start next day or has to wait on locates. Utility marking takes a few days in most areas. A temporary bypass can keep a bathroom working in the meantime.

    Commercial Sewer Lateral Work in Auburn, WA

    Apartment buildings, offices and restaurants in Auburn, WA need lateral work too. Traffic control is sometimes part of the job. Tenant notices and work windows get planned ahead.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Auburn

    A winch pulls the bursting head. The head is slightly bigger than the old pipe, so it splits clay or cast iron outward into the soil while fused HDPE trails behind it in one continuous piece. Only two pits get dug. The fused pipe has no joints, so roots cannot get in again.

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Auburn, WA

    Concrete is expensive to replace. Bursting lets us run new HDPE under a driveway, patio or mature tree by pulling it through the path of the old pipe from a launch pit to a receiving pit. We fuse the HDPE into one continuous length on the lawn. Most bursts finish in a day. You end up patching two small pits rather than a whole slab.
