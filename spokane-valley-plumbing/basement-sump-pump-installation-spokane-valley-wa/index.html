    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Spokane Valley

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    A standard basement sump pump installation usually takes 4 to 8 hours. If a new sump pit needs to be excavated, or there are unexpected challenges with plumbing or electrical access, the job might stretch to a full day. Our team works efficiently to minimize disruption to your home.

    FHN Plumbing offers a full warranty on our basement sump pump installations, covering both parts and labor. The specific warranty period will be spelled out in your service agreement. We stand behind our work and the quality of the products we install in your Spokane Valley home.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for maximum protection.

    In many areas, including Spokane Valley, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to ensure compliance with local building codes. FHN Plumbing will help you work through the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also smart to consider a replacement before it fails completely.

    Typically, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained once a year, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and confirming the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and extends the life of your pump.
