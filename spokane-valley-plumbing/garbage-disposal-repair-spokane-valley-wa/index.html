    Disposal Repair in Spokane Valley, WA: Symptom by Symptom

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Spokane Valley

    A jam usually comes from one hard object, like a peach pit, a chicken bone or a twist tie wrapped around the impeller. We free the flywheel from below with the hex key and pull the object with pliers, never a hand, then run it with water to make sure nothing else is stuck. We press the reset after clearing it. Check the trap arm if it jams again.

    Flange and Inlet Leak Repair in Spokane Valley, WA

    Leaks show up at the flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge tube or body. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. Water from the bottom usually means a failed internal seal, which makes replacement the smarter move. It is not a repairable part.

    Disposal Odor and Buildup Cleaning in Spokane Valley, WA

    Food buildup and grease on the chamber walls and under the splash guard cause most disposal smells. Many rural Washington homes rely on private wells, and some carry iron, manganese or other minerals that call for testing and treatment. Mineral scale traps the food. We clean the splash guard and chamber, check the drain for buildup, and replace a worn guard, which often clears up a smell that months of lemon peels never touched.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Spokane Valley, WA

    Fine grinding with slow draining usually means the discharge tube or the trap arm is packed. Rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits. We pull the trap, clear the tube and the arm with the right size cable, and check that the arm has at least a quarter inch of fall per foot toward the wall.

    Cost-Effective Disposal Repair in Spokane Valley, WA

    Paying half the price of a new disposal for a repair seldom pays off, and we will say so. Plenty of repairs cost little. Resetting an overload, freeing a jam, replacing a switch or resealing a flange usually costs well under the price of a replacement and keeps a younger unit running for years.

    Electrical Issues and Motor Repair in Spokane Valley

    A disposal that hums but will not grind is getting power but is stuck, while one that is silent has no power at all. They are different problems. For a silent unit we test the breaker, reset and switch, and for a humming one we check the flywheel and motor before deciding if it is worth repairing.

    Leaking Disposal Fix in Spokane Valley, WA

    A drip at the top usually means the sink flange putty has dried out. We drop the unit, pull the flange, clean off the old putty and reset it with fresh putty and new gaskets, then hang the disposal back up. That fixes most top leaks. Side leaks are usually the discharge gasket or the dishwasher hose clamp.

    InSinkErator Disposal Repair in Spokane Valley, WA

    The InSinkErator lineup runs from Badger units to the Evolution series, and we repair both. Badgers are simple single-stage units, while Evolution models have multi-stage grinding and, on some models, a separate stage that can jam on its own. Most repairs use parts from the truck. A failed motor means a new unit.
