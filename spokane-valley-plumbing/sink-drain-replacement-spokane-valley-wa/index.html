    Choosing Drain Material in Spokane Valley, WA

    PVC is the default choice under most sinks. It will not rust and costs less, while thick brass is worth it where the trap shows, like under a pedestal. ABS works the same way. We match what you have, with a proper transition where they meet.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Spokane Valley

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Lint and grit wear out thin utility tub drains and pack the trap so it never drains right. A 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout plug goes in. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Spokane Valley

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Bowls without an overflow need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, or water leaks out the slots. We see the wrong drain often. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Spokane Valley

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. Sinks below the sewer line drain through a pump, so we check its valve and discharge line during the job. Pumps fail quietly. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    Bathroom Sink Drain Replacement in Spokane Valley, WA

    Bathroom drains wear out at the pop-up. The pivot nut packing leaks, the drain body corrodes around the overflow holes, and the stopper stops sealing, so a sink that will not hold water usually needs a whole new pop-up assembly. Patching one piece rarely works. The new drain matches your faucet finish.

    Corroded Drain Replacement in Spokane Valley, WA

    Drain cleaners speed up corrosion. Caustic products sit in the bottom of a metal trap between uses and eat at the chrome plating and the thin brass underneath, which is why so many old traps we pull have pinholes right at the bottom of the bend. PVC does not have that problem. We recommend it for most cabinets.

    Hard-Water Drain Damage Repair in Spokane Valley, WA

    Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Scale grows inside pop-up bodies and on stopper rods until the linkage seizes. Forcing a scaled lift rod usually snaps the pivot and leaves the stopper stuck, with a narrowed tailpiece slowing the drain. The scaled parts all come out. Smooth PVC sheds buildup better.

    P-Trap Replacement in Spokane Valley

    Thin chrome traps are the ones we replace most. They rust from the inside, the plating flakes off, and a slip nut that will not loosen often twists the whole trap apart, so we cut it out and install heavy-wall PVC or polished brass that matches the room. Then it gets a full flow test. Total time is often under an hour.
