    Sewer Repair Methods for Pasco, WA Homes

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Pasco, WA

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Then the sewer smell shows up. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, so we check the vent any time the drains act up but the main line looks clean. We clear it from the roof or replace the damaged section.

    Cracked and Broken Pipe Repair in Pasco

    Clay and cast iron crack from settling soil, tree roots and vehicles parked over the line. The crack lets soil in and sewage out. We excavate at the marked spot, cut out the damaged length and install new Schedule 40 PVC with shielded stainless couplings rated for the transition between materials, then test the repair with water and video before closing the hole. Figure on a single day for a typical spot repair.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Pasco

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, and once the camera shows failures along the whole run, patching it stops being worth the money. PVC goes in, usually end to end. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Emergency Sewer Repair in Pasco

    Raw sewage on a basement floor is not a next-week problem. We answer at night and on weekends. The first goal on an emergency call is to get the line flowing, usually with a cable or jetter, and then camera it to find out whether you have a clog or a structural failure that will back up again tomorrow. The real repair may be scheduled for the next morning. Until then, we tell you which fixtures are safe to use.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection & Repair in Pasco, WA

    Sewer smell outside the house points to a leak. A lush stripe over the line is another clue. We confirm it on camera, look for cracks, open joints or a broken cleanout riser, and locate it on the surface to keep the excavation tight. A small crack can sometimes be fixed with a short sectional liner, with no digging at all. Larger breaks get cut out and replaced.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in Pasco, WA

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. The old pipe gets jetted clean before anything else. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Pasco

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. Crushed pipe cannot be lined. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. We see this most in older neighborhoods with clay pipe. The dig is staged so the house is without a sewer for as little time as possible.

    Pipe Burst & Collapse Repair in Pasco, WA

    A collapsed sewer pipe stops all flow at the point of failure. The whole house loses its drains. Collapses usually come from corroded cast iron, crushed clay under a driveway or tar-paper pipe that has finally flattened, and the camera tells us exactly where the pipe gave way and how deep it sits. We dig at the mark and replace the broken length with PVC. Lining cannot fix a full collapse.
