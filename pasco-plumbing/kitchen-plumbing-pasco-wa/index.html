    Recurring Kitchen Clogs in Pasco, WA

    Grease, rice, pasta and coffee grounds build up in the trap and branch line over months, not days. We clear the line with a cable sized for kitchen pipe and can camera it if the clog keeps coming back. The pipe is sometimes the cause. A low spot in the line or a drain arm without enough fall will collect grease however often it is cleared.

    Grease Trap Installation and Cleaning in Pasco

    Grease traps are sized by flow, and a three-compartment sink typically calls for a trap rated at 20 gallons per minute or more. We set the trap level, install the flow control fitting and vent it so it does not siphon. Cleaning schedules depend on volume. A kitchen running fryers all day may need service every couple of weeks.

    InSinkErator Installation and Service in Pasco

    InSinkErator units use the Quick Lock mount, which makes trading one InSinkErator for another a quick job. We install the Badger line for basic use and the Evolution series when you want more grinding stages and less noise. We replace worn mounts. We reset the sink flange in new putty, knock out the dishwasher plug when there is a dishwasher, and match the cord to the outlet.

    Dishwasher and Ice Maker Lines in Pasco

    A dishwasher needs a hot supply with its own shutoff, a drain to the disposal or a wye tailpiece, and an air gap on the sink deck or a high loop fastened under the counter, depending on local code. Ice makers need a cold line. We run braided stainless or copper to the fridge with a quarter-turn shutoff, never the thin plastic tubing that splits behind the fridge.

    Burst Supply Lines in Pasco, WA

    Plastic supply lines with plastic nuts crack more often than braided stainless, especially after years of pressure swings. A failed line under the sink can put several gallons a minute into the cabinet and the floor below before anyone notices it. We replace both sides. The second line is just as old as the one that failed.

    Kitchen Faucet Repair and Replacement in Pasco

    Most single-handle kitchen faucets drip because of a worn cartridge, and Moen, Delta and Kohler all sell the part by model. We bring common cartridges. When the body is corroded or the pull-down hose is leaking under the sink, a new faucet usually costs close to what the parts and labor would.

    Disposal Installation and Upgrades in Pasco

    Most families do fine with a 1/2 horsepower unit, though heavy cooks and big families get more from 3/4 or 1 HP. Each install gets a new mounting assembly, a new discharge tube and a punched dishwasher knockout when there is a dishwasher. Every unit runs with water before we leave.     Frequently Asked Questions About Kitchen Plumbing in Pasco

    Trap and supply line fixes are usually $125 to $300. Expect $250 to $650 for a disposal with the unit, $350 to $900 for a kitchen sink install, and about $1,500 and up for a commercial grease trap. Prices are flat.

    Grease, coffee grounds, pasta and rice pile up in the drain line. Scale from hard water makes the buildup grab faster. A professional cleaning plus a few better habits at the disposal usually stops it for good, unless the line has a sag or poor slope.

    Repair covers jams, tripped resets, loose mounts and leaking sink flanges. Replacement is the answer when the motor burns out, the grinding chamber cracks, or water leaks from the bottom of the housing, because those parts are not sold separately. We tell you which one it is.

    Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard and Pfister faucets are all ones we install and repair, plus InSinkErator, Waste King and Moen disposals and most other supply house brands. Parts for the common models ride on the truck. Store-brand faucets are trickier because their cartridges are often not sold on their own.

    Under-sink traps usually need cleaning every few weeks, while large interceptors are pumped every one to three months. Your local utility may set a required schedule.

    Most commercial kitchens clean every one to three months, though a busy fryer kitchen may need it every few weeks. Homes rarely need one. A common rule is to pump it before grease and solids take up about 25 percent of the trap, and some sewer districts set the interval for you.

    Figure on two to four hours for a standard sink swap when the counter opening fits and the drain lines are in good shape. Undermounts take longer. If the countertop has to be cut, the cabinet modified or the drain arm moved, the job can run most of a day.
