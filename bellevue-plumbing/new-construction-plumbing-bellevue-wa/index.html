    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Bellevue

    Most new single-family homes in the Bellevue area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Gas piping and tankless heaters push it up. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling fixtures or sewer odor point to venting or slope problems. We can camera the line from the cleanout to look for a belly or a bad joint while the builder warranty still applies. Construction debris is common.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It flexes and needs fewer fittings in the walls than copper. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. Copper still shows up at water heaters.

    We can run a dedicated return line during rough-in. Hot water then reaches distant bathrooms in seconds. Compared with a crossover valve at the sink, a dedicated return keeps the cold line cold and works faster. The pump needs an outlet.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Manufacturer warranties cover the fixtures themselves. PEX and water heater warranties can run ten years or longer if the matching fittings are used, so we keep each system one brand.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. Top-out gets inspected before insulation, and the final comes after fixtures are set and running. Some add a gas test.

    Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. Ground movement puts stress on pipes under a slab. Our crews bed drains in sand or gravel, sleeve pipes through the slab and run PEX water services where permitted. Sleeves are cheap.

    Some jurisdictions let owner-builders plumb a home they will live in. Permits and inspections still apply. Inspectors hold owner-builders to the same code as licensed plumbers, and a failed underground test can cost more in delay than hiring the phase out would have cost. Gas piping often requires a license.
