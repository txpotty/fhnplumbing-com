    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Bellevue, WA

    The anode rod, often called the sacrificial rod, is vital for preventing corrosion inside your hot water tank. Water chemistry varies from one neighborhood to the next, and it changes the rod's lifespan. Our technicians inspect the anode rod for wear and recommend replacement if it's significantly corroded, typically every 3 to 5 years. Replacing a worn anode rod is a cost-effective way to protect your tank from rust and leaks, sparing you a full tank replacement.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Bellevue

    The cost of hot water tank maintenance in Bellevue, WA generally ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age and specific services required. This normally includes flushing, anode rod inspection and safety valve checks. We provide upfront pricing before any work begins.

    A typical hot water tank maintenance service usually takes about 1 to 2 hours. This includes draining, flushing, inspection and any minor adjustments or part replacements like an anode rod.

    Many manufacturers, including Rheem, A.O. Smith and Bradford White, expect regular maintenance and can reject claims for sediment damage. Keeping service records protects your warranty.

    FHN Plumbing stands behind our work. While maintenance services usually don't have long-term warranties like new installations, we guarantee our workmanship for a set period. Any parts replaced during maintenance will carry their manufacturer's warranty.

    Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Mineral content adds to sediment buildup in hot water tanks, which reduces efficiency and can damage heating elements or tank linings over time. Regular flushing is particularly important in hard water areas.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which matters most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it lengthens the lifespan of your unit.

    Most flushes and inspections take 45 minutes to 90 minutes. Heavily scaled tanks or anode replacements in tight closets can take a bit longer.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to inefficient operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service prevents these costly and dangerous issues.
