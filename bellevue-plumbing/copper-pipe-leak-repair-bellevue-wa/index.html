    Copper Pipe Joint Leak Repair in Bellevue, WA

    Joints leak from poor original soldering, thermal expansion or a house that settles. Crawlspaces and basements are both common in Washington homes, and a damp crawlspace is often the first sign of a drainage or plumbing problem. Look for white crust. We inspect each joint near the leak for weeping or mineral buildup, then clean, reflux and resolder the joint, or replace the fitting entirely with a new sweat or press fitting. Every joint gets tested.

    Corrosion Leak Repair in Bellevue, WA

    Water can be hard on copper over time. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is generally soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Thinning walls lead to leaks. We look at corrosion patterns on the pipe we remove, because the location and shape of the pits tell us whether water chemistry, velocity or electrical current is doing the damage.     Split Copper Line Repair in Bellevue, WA

    A copper line can split from a freeze, a settling foundation or a hit from a nail. Splits leak hard. Our plumber isolates the water source, cuts out the split and solders in new pipe, while checking nearby pipe for damage from the same cause. Insulation goes on exposed runs.

    Hot Water Copper Line Repair in Bellevue, WA

    Hot water lines corrode faster than cold lines. Temperature drives it. Recirculation pumps that run around the clock push water through the copper at high velocity and wear it from the inside, which is why we often add a timer to the pump after fixing the leak. It slows the next pinhole.

    Accessible copper leak repairs typically cost $250 to $650. Walls cost more.     Every repair is warrantied. We write the warranty down. Our workmanship warranty covers the repair, while the parts carry separate manufacturer coverage. You see the terms first.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Worn pipe is usually excluded. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Ask your insurer. We provide documentation.

    Wall thickness. Type K is the heaviest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, and Type M has the thinnest wall, which is why it tends to pinhole first in older homes. Our repairs use Type L.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. Temporary fixes fail. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, since a failed patch at full pressure can soak a floor in minutes. Treat it as a stopgap.

    A bill that climbs for no reason is a big one. Musty smells count. Damp spots on walls or ceilings, the sound of running water with every fixture off and warm spots on a slab floor all point to a leak you cannot see yet. The meter will confirm it.

    An accessible pinhole or a single joint takes one to four hours. Slab leaks take longer. Leaks under a slab or a repipe can take a full day or more, with access and rerouting as the main things that stretch it out. The quote includes a time estimate.
