    Deep Well Pump Repair in Bellevue

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually.     Submersible Well Pump Repair in Bellevue

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. Testing starts at the surface. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Bellevue

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. Shaft seal leaks and worn impellers come next. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Bellevue

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Sensor failures and drive errors are the usual problems. FHN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Bellevue, WA well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Bellevue

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. We check the precharge with the tank drained. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Bellevue

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.
