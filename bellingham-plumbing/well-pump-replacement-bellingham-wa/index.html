    Common signs include no water from your faucets, weak or inconsistent water pressure, unusual noises from the well or pressure tank area, short cycling where the pump turns on and off frequently, or cloudy or muddy water.     Regular maintenance, such as checking your well's water level, keeping the pressure tank air charge correct and fixing any leaks promptly, can extend your pump's life. Avoiding heavy water use and installing a good quality sediment filter can also reduce wear and tear on the pump.

    Common signs of a failing well pump include no water, sputtering faucets, reduced water pressure, discolored water or surprisingly high electricity bills. If your pump runs constantly or cycles on and off too frequently, these are also signs that replacement might be necessary.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job involving electrical wiring, heavy lifting and specialized tools, especially for submersible pumps. It is highly recommended to hire a professional plumber for safety, proper installation and compliance with all codes, avoiding costly mistakes.
