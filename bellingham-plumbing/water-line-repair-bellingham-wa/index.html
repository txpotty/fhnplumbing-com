    What Our Water Line Service Covers in Bellingham, WA

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Bellingham

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Bellingham

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer homes in Bellingham usually use PEX supply lines, and many older houses have been partially repiped over the years. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Bellingham, WA

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Fittings in the box loosen with ground movement and traffic. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, since the utility is in charge of the meter and the pipe feeding it. When it is on your side, we repair it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Underground Leak Detection in Bellingham, WA

    A ground microphone picks up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe, even through a couple of feet of dirt. Quiet leaks are harder. For those we push a hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas into the drained line and sniff for it at the surface, which finds leaks the microphone cannot hear. Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. Once the spot is marked, you get a written repair price before we dig.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Bellingham

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Bellingham, WA

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Many rural Washington homes rely on private wells, and some carry iron, manganese or other minerals that call for testing and treatment. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Usually a one-day job.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Bellingham

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner owns the line to the house. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, while the new pipe is pulled into the path it just opened. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers add time for cutting the slab, restoring the surface and waiting on the inspector to show up. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, and that coverage usually pays for the line repair. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    Most replacements get Type K copper or PEX. For pulled lines we use HDPE. Copper lasts a very long time and resists punctures, and PEX flexes with the soil, tolerates freezing better and goes in faster. We recommend one based on your soil, line length, budget and what your local code allows in Bellingham.
