    Residential Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Bellingham

    Residential electric tankless installs start with counting fixtures that run at the same time. A household running two showers at once in cold weather usually needs a 27 kW unit on three 40 amp breakers. One bath needs less. Our usual brands are Rheem, EcoSmart and Stiebel Eltron.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Upgrades in Bellingham, WA

    Upgrading from a 50 gallon electric tank to tankless is mostly an electrical project. Where the tank used one 30 amp circuit, a whole-home tankless often needs two or three 40 amp circuits. The water side is simple. Old tank removal is included.

    Rheem Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Bellingham

    Rheem's RTEX line covers most of the whole-home electric tankless units we install. The RTEX-18 runs on two 40 amp breakers, while the RTEX-24 and RTEX-27 need three, so we match the model to your panel before ordering. They mount flat on the wall. Water, temperature and leak checks finish the job.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Bellingham

    Hard water builds scale on tankless heating elements, which cuts efficiency and can trigger error codes. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is typically soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. We recommend a softener or a scale inhibitor ahead of the unit, and every install gets service valves so the heater can be flushed with a descaling solution once a year. Flushing takes an hour.

    Cost Comparison: Electric Tankless vs. Traditional in Bellingham, WA

    Upfront, electric tankless usually costs more than a tank because of the electrical work. Over 20 years, a tankless unit may outlast two tank heaters and avoids standby loss the whole time, so the total cost can come out close or lower depending on use. Usage decides it. We give you a side-by-side before you decide.

    Electrical Panel Review and Upgrades in Bellingham

    Before any electric tankless install, we look hard at the panel. We check service size, free slots and current load, since large units can need over 100 amps. Some homes need an upgrade. We bring in an electrician when needed.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless Electric in Bellingham

    Switching from tank to tankless electric usually takes panel work and some re-piping. New circuits and heavier wire are typical, and the water lines are rerouted to the new spot. We coordinate the electrician. You get floor space back.

    Whole-Home Electric Tankless Systems in Bellingham, WA

    Whole-home coverage in Bellingham, WA means one unit big enough for your busiest hour. Sizing uses peak fixtures and the coldest water, and sometimes two smaller units beat one big one. Every tap stays hot. We plan it before quoting.
