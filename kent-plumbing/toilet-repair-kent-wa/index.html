    Toilet Flush Mechanism Repair in Kent, WA

    If your toilet isn't flushing properly, it could be due to issues with the flush chain, handle, flapper, or fill valve. A weak flush might mean the tank isn't filling correctly, while a loose handle might not be engaging the flapper. Our plumbers are skilled at diagnosing these internal tank problems, replacing worn or broken components like flush valves, fill valves, and toilet handles to restore full and effective flushing power.

    Running Toilet Fixes in Kent

    Leaking Toilet Repair in Kent

    Toilet leaks can cause serious water damage to your bathroom floor, subfloor, and even the ceiling below. Leaks can start from the base of the toilet, the tank, or the supply line. Common culprits include a worn-out wax ring, loose bolts, or cracked porcelain. We thoroughly inspect all potential leak points, replacing seals, tightening connections, or recommending bowl replacement if the porcelain is damaged, preventing further water intrusion.

    Leaking Toilet Base Repair in Kent, WA

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange, install a repair ring or new flange if needed and reset the bowl on a fresh seal.     Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Repair in Kent

    Most simple toilet repairs, like a new flapper or fill valve, run about $125 to $275 including parts and labor. Resetting a toilet on a new wax ring is usually $175 to $350, and flange repairs can bring the total to $300 to $500. We give you a flat price before starting.

    The most common causes are a worn flapper, a mineral-crusted flush valve seat or a fill valve set too high so water spills into the overflow tube. A chain that is too short can also hold the flapper open. A quick inspection usually pinpoints the cause in minutes.

    Most standard toilet repairs, such as fixing a running toilet or replacing internal tank components, can be completed within 1 to 2 hours. More complex issues like clearing a tough main line clog or replacing a wax ring might take longer, potentially 2 to 4 hours.

    If your toilet is overflowing, immediately reach behind the toilet and turn the small valve on the water supply line clockwise to shut off the water. This will stop the water from continuously filling the bowl. Then, try to clear any visible blockage with a plunger and call a licensed plumber like FHN Plumbing for assistance.

    If the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well, a repair is generally the smart choice. If it uses 3.5 gallons or more per flush, clogs often or has a cracked tank, replacing it with a 1.28 gallon WaterSense model often makes more sense.

    Most flapper, fill valve and supply line repairs take 20 to 45 minutes. Pulling and resetting a toilet typically takes about an hour, and flange repairs can add another 30 to 60 minutes.

    Watch for phantom refills, hissing from the tank, a toilet that rocks, stains or soft flooring at the base and frequent clogs. Catching these early keeps a small repair from turning into a floor replacement.

    Swapping a flapper is a reasonable DIY job if you match the right size and the flush valve seat is smooth. Base leaks, flange repairs and cracked tanks are better left to a plumber because a bad reset can cause hidden water damage to the subfloor.
