    Custom Shower System Installation in Kent, WA

    Multi-head showers depend on valve capacity. A standard pressure-balance valve can feed one head, but rain heads, body jets and handhelds need a thermostatic valve with separate volume controls and supply lines sized up to 3/4 inch. Layout comes first. The water heater gets checked for capacity too.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Kent

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Then we test. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.

    Tub and Shower Combo Installation in Kent

    Setting a tub is mostly about level and support. Steel and acrylic tubs need a mortar bed or foam under them, and cast iron needs a level ledger, so the tub sits flat and the caulk joint at the wall does not crack. Then the valve. Spout and head go at the heights you choose, and the overflow gasket gets a water test.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Kent

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. A wood floor gets dropped joists or a recessed pan, while a slab gets cut and lowered so the floor flows straight in. A linear drain often works best. We plan the slope with your contractor before demo.

    Shower Stall Installation in Kent

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. We set fiberglass and acrylic bases in a bed of mortar or setting compound so the floor of the unit does not flex underfoot, since flex is what cracks a base within a couple of years. Then the drain goes in. We check the flow at the head once the trim is on.

    Shower Upgrades Without a Full Remodel in Kent

    Not every upgrade needs demolition. A new pan, a tub conversion or a new valve and trim can change the bathroom without a gut job. You see each option's price. Most of these finish in a few days.
