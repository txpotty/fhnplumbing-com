    Freeze and Heat Protection for Backflow Preventers in Kent, WA

    Above-ground backflow assemblies sit in the open through summer sun and winter cold. A single hard freeze can crack a brass body or split a bonnet, and years of UV make plastic test cock caps and covers brittle. Covers help a lot.     Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Kent

    Annual residential testing runs $75 to $150 per assembly. A rebuild usually costs $150 to $450. Residential installs run about $300 to $1,200, while a commercial RPZ is often $1,500 to $5,000 installed with its drain and enclosure.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Brands include Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco, along with Apollo and Zurn models that show up mostly on larger commercial lines. Common kits ride along.

    Once a year is the usual requirement. Some providers require it after installation, repair or relocation as well. Your provider's notice will list the deadline, and we can usually test within a few days of seeing it, sometimes sooner.

    A brass assembly often lasts 10 to 20 years, sometimes longer. The rubber parts inside wear out much sooner, which is why rebuild kits exist and why most failed tests are fixed without replacing the body. Cold snaps are hard on them.

    Water dripping or running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. Low pressure at the house, a cracked bonnet after a freeze or water that tastes or smells off can also point to the assembly. Get it tested soon.

    Installation and repair should be left to a licensed plumber who holds tester certification. Installing the wrong device, mounting a PVB too low or burying an RPZ in a pit can each fail inspection and leave the water unprotected. Providers can fine for it.

    A standard test usually takes 20 to 45 minutes per assembly. We tell you before shutting off the water for the few minutes the test needs. A rebuild adds about an hour.

    In most cases, yes, installing or replacing an assembly in Kent needs a plumbing permit from the local building department. The inspector confirms the device fits the hazard and is mounted at the correct height with room to test and drain. Permits are on us.
