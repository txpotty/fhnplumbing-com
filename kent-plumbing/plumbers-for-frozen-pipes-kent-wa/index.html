    Residential Frozen Pipe Thawing in Kent

    A faucet that trickles or goes dry on a cold morning points to ice somewhere in its supply line. A thermal camera shows the frozen section. Once we know where it is, we thaw it with controlled heat while the faucet stays open, so the melting water has a way out and pressure cannot build behind the ice.

    Pipe Insulation and Heat Cable Installation in Kent, WA

    Insulation slows heat loss, but it does not add heat. For truly exposed runs, we add heat cable. Self-regulating cable on a GFCI outlet, covered with insulation, keeps the pipe above freezing.

    Heat Cable for Exposed Pipes in Kent

    Some pipes cannot be rerouted or insulated enough on their own, like a supply line under a manufactured home or a pipe through an unheated porch. Those get heat cable. Old-style constant-wattage heat tape can overheat and is a known fire risk, so we install self-regulating cable listed for the pipe material and replace worn or brittle heat tape we find.

    Preventive Pipe Insulation in Kent

    Foam pipe insulation is cheap and stops most freezes in attics and crawlspaces. Fit matters. We size the sleeve to the pipe, tape every seam and miter the corners so there are no gaps at elbows and tees, since a single bare fitting is often exactly where a line freezes first.

    Post-Thaw Pipe Inspection and Repair in Kent, WA

    A pipe that froze and thawed without leaking may still be damaged. We pressure test it to find out. With the line isolated and a gauge on it, even a hairline split shows up as a pressure drop within minutes, and finding it now beats finding it later as a stain on the ceiling.

    Frozen Water Line Diagnosis in Kent, WA

    Finding the frozen spot is the hard part. Thermal cameras make it faster. Fixture checks plus a thermal scan usually narrow the ice to a few feet of pipe.

    Time matters once a line freezes, because the longer ice sits, the more pressure builds behind it. Open the faucet on that line now. Most frozen lines flow again within an hour of arrival, and every one gets a pressure test.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Kent

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Kent usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Freeze-proofing is cheaper. Insulating a crawlspace, adding heat cable to the worst run, and swapping outdoor faucets for frost-free models usually costs a few hundred dollars, which is less than a single burst pipe repair.

    Yes, every repair includes a written workmanship warranty. Your invoice lists the terms. If the repair we made leaks during the warranty period, we come back and fix it, and if a different section of the same exposed run splits in a later freeze, we will quote a permanent fix for the whole run.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. That pipe is still exposed. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger jobs can. Repiping, rerouting or replacing a backflow device may need one, which we handle.

    Plumbers in Washington must hold a state certification, and water heater replacements and repiping typically require a permit. Most codes call for protecting pipes in unheated spaces and limit supply lines in outside walls. We follow the adopted local version. Inspectors check it.

    Pipe location matters more than brand. Copper and galvanized split first. Metal pipe in cold spots splits most, and PEX often survives except at the fittings.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Insulation and air sealing do most of the work. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.
