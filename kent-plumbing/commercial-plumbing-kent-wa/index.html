    Grease Trap and Interceptor Repair in Kent, WA

    Food service sites need a grease trap or interceptor to keep fats, oils and grease out of the public sewer, and pretreatment inspectors check them. Odor is the first warning.     Commercial Water Heater Repair and Installation in Kent, WA

    A commercial water heater is judged by recovery rate, the gallons it can reheat per hour, not by tank size alone. Undersized heaters run cold by mid-shift. Our techs work on AO Smith, Rheem, Bradford White and State commercial heaters and check flue draft, gas valves and the T and P valve each visit. New installs get sized from your peak hour demand. Proper sizing is what keeps a busy kitchen in hot water all night.

    Commercial Drain Cleaning and Hydro-Jetting in Kent

    Commercial drains carry grease, paper towels and food waste in volumes no house ever sees. A cable just pokes a hole. Our jetters push water at 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle that scours the full pipe wall, which clears 4-inch and 6-inch lines back to bare pipe. A jetted line stays open far longer than a cabled one. We see the most benefit in restaurants, schools and multi-family stacks.

    Slab Leak Detection for Businesses in Kent

    A slab leak under a store or office floor often shows up as a warm spot, a running water meter with everything off, or a crack in floor tile. Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. We confirm it with a meter test and pressure test, then use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak within a foot or two. Then we decide. A spot repair through the floor makes sense for some, and an overhead reroute makes more sense for others. You get a price on each option.

    Backflow Testing and Certification in Kent

    Each backflow test takes about 30 minutes per assembly and requires a short water shutoff to that line. Plan for a brief outage. Our tester opens each test cock in order, records the check valve and relief valve readings on a calibrated gauge and compares them against the pass limits printed on the test form. Passing devices get tagged. The provider gets the report from us, and we keep a copy for your next test.
