    Sump Pump Services in Kent, WA

    A sump pump keeps groundwater out of basements and crawlspaces.     French Drain Installation in Kent

    A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with perforated pipe that collects groundwater and carries it away. We wrap the gravel in filter fabric so silt does not clog it. It needs steady fall to an outlet, and the outlet gets a grate to keep critters out.

    Outdoor & Storm Drain Cleaning in Kent

    Area drains, catch basins and driveway trench drains fill with leaves and silt. A buried grate sends water across the yard, and the pipe under it fills with mud. Basins get cleaned out and the pipe gets jetted. Fall cleaning helps most.

    Channel and Trench Drains in Kent, WA

    Driveways and patios that slope toward the house can send runoff straight into a garage or a door threshold. A trench drain cut into the concrete stops it. The channel goes into a saw-cut in the slab with a traffic-rated grate and solid pipe to an outlet.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Kent, WA

    Recurring drain trouble calls for a camera, not another cable run. The camera records the whole line, and its locator lets us mark the problem spot and depth above ground. You get the video. Buyers often order one before closing.

    Hydro-Jetting Services in Kent

    A hydro jetter sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scours the full pipe wall. Unlike a cable, the jet cleans the whole pipe wall, not just a hole through the clog. Grease lines benefit most. We camera the line first to make sure the pipe is sound enough to jet.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Kent

    Most basic drain clearing in Kent, WA runs $150 to $500. A main line costs more than a sink. Hydro jetting runs higher than cabling because of the equipment and time, and sewer line repair can range from about $1,500 for a spot repair to well over $8,000 for a full replacement with digging.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. Trench drains take more skill. They need proper slope and a legal outlet, and digging without an 811 locate is risky.

    Store-bought cleaners rarely clear a real clog. They can make things worse. The chemicals sit in the trap, heat up and can damage older pipe or soften glue joints, and if the clog does not clear, the plumber now has to open a trap full of caustic liquid. A hand auger is the better tool.

    Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. A sewer smell in the yard or basement comes next. In the yard, standing water a day after rain or washed-out mulch means the water has nowhere to go.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Bigger jobs take longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.

    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. Clean them when they slow down. Root-prone sewers and grease-heavy kitchens benefit from cleaning every one to two years.

    Watch for water pooling near the house after storms. Mulch washing onto the walk is another sign. A damp crawlspace or white crust on the foundation means water is reaching the house.

    Roots usually enter through an existing crack or joint, then widen it as they grow. Clay pipe can split over time. A root mass can also push a joint out of line, creating an offset that catches waste, and in bad cases the pipe collapses and has to be dug up or lined from the inside.
