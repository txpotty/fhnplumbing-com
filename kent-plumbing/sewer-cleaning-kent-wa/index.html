    Sewer Cleaning Versus Sewer Repair in Kent, WA

    Cleaning cannot fix a broken pipe. It only removes what is inside. When a line keeps backing up, we put the cost of regular cleaning next to the cost of a repair so you can see which makes sense, with the numbers in writing.

    Root Masses in Kent Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Clay joints let roots in. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Kent

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Both have weak spots. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Preventative Sewer Maintenance in Kent

    Roots come back after cutting. Usually within a year or so. A scheduled cleaning every year or two keeps roots and grease from closing the line and lets you plan around it.

    Residential Sewer Cleaning in Kent

    Household sewers clog slowly. Grease, hair and wipes build up over months. Debris catches at a rough joint or a sag, and every load of laundry adds to it until the line closes, and we clear it with a sectional cable or a small jetter sized for the pipe.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Kent

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. Cabling just opens a hole. Water at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI strips grease, scale and fine roots off the inside of the line and flushes it all toward the street, which leaves the pipe close to its original diameter.

    After-Hours Sewer Backups in Kent

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Kent, WA

    Grease and scale are jetting work. A cable only scratches them. The rear jets drive the hose forward while the spray scours the wall, so the line ends up much closer to its original size.
