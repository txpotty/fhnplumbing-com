    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Kent

    Clearing a clog usually runs $150 to $400. Pipe repairs cost more. Main line repairs can be anywhere from $800 to $5,000 or more, especially when the pipe is buried and has to be excavated to reach the damaged section. The written quote comes after diagnosis.

    Every repair is warrantied. Our labor carries a written warranty. Replacement parts carry their manufacturer warranties, and we explain what is and is not covered at the time of the visit. No fine print.

    Clearing a simple clog takes an hour or two. Repairs run longer. Replacing a section of main line or opening a wall to swap a cast iron stack may run a day or longer with access and inspections setting the pace. We give you a timeline with the price.

    Tightening a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is fair game. Hand snakes are fine too. Anything inside a wall, under a floor or involving cast iron, or a repeat leak at the same joint, should go to a plumber. Skip the caustic cleaners.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Usually it is buildup. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge catches debris over and over, even after the line is cleared. A camera shows which one you have.

    Hydro-jetting scours the pipe with water at several thousand psi. It cleans wall to wall. A snake only punches a hole through a clog, but a jet nozzle strips buildup off the entire pipe wall. Fragile pipe gets checked before jetting.

    Small fixture drain repairs usually do not. Larger jobs often do. Replacing drain stacks, relocating drains or adding new lines often needs a permit, depending on the city and how much of the system is being changed. The permit is on us if needed.

    A dry trap is the most common cause. Unused fixtures dry out. A cracked drain fitting, a failed wax ring under the toilet or a blocked vent can also let sewer gas into the room, and we track it down with a smoke test or a close visual check. Then we seal it.
