    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Federal Way

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. Forgetting the plug floods the dishwasher. We also install an air gap or high loop, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher while the disposal is draining.

    Switching From Batch-Feed to Continuous-Feed in Federal Way, WA

    A continuous-feed unit needs a switch, which a batch-feed kitchen may not have. That can be a wall switch, an air switch button on the sink deck or a switched outlet under the sink, and the choice depends on how hard it is to run wire to the spot you want. Air switches avoid new wiring in the wall. The plumbing side is usually a straight swap.

    Same-Day Disposal Installation in Federal Way, WA

    When the old disposal dies and the sink is full of water, we can usually install a new one the same day.     Commercial Disposal Installation in Federal Way

    Commercial disposals run 1 to 5 horsepower and usually need a dedicated circuit, a control panel and a water solenoid. We set the unit on the mounting collar, run the drain to the grease trap where the local rules require it, and wire the controls so the water runs whenever the motor does. Health codes vary. The local rule gets confirmed before ordering.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Federal Way

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. Power needs a check too. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.

    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Federal Way

    These disposals run only with the stopper lid seated, which adds safety for families with kids. They need no wall switch, so they are a good choice for kitchens where adding a switch would mean cutting into finished drywall or tile backsplash. The drain hookup is the usual setup.
