    Supply and Drain Leaks Under the Slab in Federal Way, WA

    A supply leak and a drain leak show different signs. Supply leaks are pressurized, so they move the meter, warm the floor on the hot side and run whether or not anyone uses water. Drain leaks only leak when water goes down, showing up as smells, sluggish drains or wet spots near bathrooms. We test both kinds in your Federal Way, WA home.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Detection in Federal Way

    Hot lines leak under slabs more often than cold ones. Heat speeds up corrosion, and the pipe expands and contracts every time someone showers, rubbing against concrete and gravel until it wears through. Warm floors and a water heater that runs nonstop are the signs. Gas or electric bills may jump too.

    Camera Inspection for Under-Slab Drains in Federal Way, WA

    Drain lines under the slab can leak too. A broken cast iron or PVC drain will not move the water meter, but it can cause odors, soggy floors and slow drains, which is why we push a camera through to look for cracks, bellies and pulled joints. You see the footage yourself before any repair talk starts in your Federal Way home.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in Federal Way

    Professional slab leak detection in Federal Way, WA typically runs $250 to $700, depending on how hard the leak is to find and how long it takes. Repair costs are separate. Those vary a lot, based on where the leak sits and whether we spot repair, reroute or repipe.

    A water line leak is pressurized, so it runs constantly, moves the meter and can warm the floor if it is on the hot side. Drain leaks are different. They only leak when fixtures drain, which shows up as odors, slow drains or wet floors.

    You can spot the signs. Pinpointing it takes equipment. Acoustic and thermal tools are what separate a small opening from a torn-up floor, so guessing often puts a hole in the wrong spot.

    High water bills, warm floor spots and the sound of running water with fixtures off are the big three. Cracks matter too. Wet flooring, low pressure and musty smells along baseboards can also mean a slab leak.

    It can. Water under the slab swells or washes out soil. Over months, that can lead to cracks, settling and a structural repair that costs far more than fixing the pipe.

    Soft copper under older slabs tops the list, since it corrodes from the water side and wears where it rubs concrete. Plastic lines are not immune. Plastic tends to fail at fittings or from heat, while old galvanized rusts through.

    Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. Water chemistry can also thin copper from the inside. Pipes grow with hot water and wear against gravel, and over years that wear opens pinholes and cracks.

    Reroutes and repipes often require one. A small spot repair might not. Rules vary by city and county, and we handle the permit, the inspection and the paperwork whenever one is needed.
