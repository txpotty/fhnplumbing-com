    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Vancouver

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Vancouver

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    Emergency PEX Pipe Repair & Installation in Vancouver, WA

    PEX rarely fails on its own. When it does, the cause is usually a nail or screw driven through it, a rodent chewing on an attic run, or a fitting that was never crimped all the way. Our trucks stock half-inch to one-inch pipe and fittings. Most emergency PEX repairs in Vancouver, WA are a cut-and-splice job that takes under an hour once the drywall is open.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Vancouver, WA

    With PEX-A, the tech slips an expansion ring onto the pipe and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting is pushed in while the pipe is open, and the plastic's memory shrinks it back around the barb, sealing tighter during the first minute. Cold weather slows it. We let joints set before testing.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Vancouver

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. That takes seconds. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Vancouver, WA

    Think of a manifold like an electrical panel, but for water lines. Each fixture gets its own line from a central header, and each line gets its own valve, so you can shut off one toilet without killing water to the kitchen. You buy more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.
