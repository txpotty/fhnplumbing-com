    Electrical Issues & Motor Repair in Vancouver

    If your garbage disposal hums but doesn't grind, or doesn't turn on at all, it could be an electrical issue or a motor problem. We check circuit breakers, reset buttons, and internal wiring. For motor failures, we assess if a repair is feasible or if replacement is the more cost-effective solution, always keeping your safety first in Vancouver.

    Noisy or Grinding Disposal Repair in Vancouver

    Loud metal-on-metal sounds may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. We inspect the chamber, tighten the mounting ring and remove debris. If the bearings are failing, the noise will continue. In that case, we can quote a quieter replacement unit.

    Leaking Garbage Disposal Fix in Vancouver, WA

    Leaks from a garbage disposal can stem from various points, including loose sink flanges, worn-out seals, cracked housing, or pipe connections. We meticulously inspect all potential leak sources, replacing faulty gaskets or tightening connections as needed. Addressing leaks promptly prevents water damage to your cabinets and flooring in Vancouver, WA.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Vancouver, WA

    A disposal that grinds but drains slowly often has a clogged discharge tube or trap arm. Grease, rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits. We clear the line with the right tools and check for proper slope. We also explain what not to put the disposal.

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Vancouver

    A humming disposal that will not spin usually has a jam. Bones, fruit pits, glass or utensils can wedge the flywheel. We cut power, use a hex wrench on the bottom of the unit to free the flywheel and remove the object. Then we test the motor and press the reset button if needed.

    Leaking Disposal Repair in Vancouver, WA

    Leaks can come from the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, the discharge tube or the disposal body itself. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty, and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. A leak from the bottom of the unit usually means the internal seal has failed. In that case, replacement is the smarter option.
