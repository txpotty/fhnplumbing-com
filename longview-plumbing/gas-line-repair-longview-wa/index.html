    Appliance Connector and Shutoff Repair in Longview

    Old flexible connectors, especially uncoated brass ones, crack and leak with age. New coated stainless connectors go on, sized to the appliance, with a reachable shutoff valve if the old one is stuck or missing. This is a quick fix. Furnaces, water heaters, ranges and dryers each need a sound connection, and most need a drip leg before the appliance valve.

    Emergency Gas Line Repair in Longview

    If you smell gas, leave the building, skip the light switches and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Then call us. Once the utility has shut off the meter and made the area safe, we locate the leak, make the repair and run the pressure test needed before service can be restored. Pilots get relit once gas is back. The truck carries enough pipe and fittings to finish most emergency jobs in one trip.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Longview

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Crawlspaces and basements are both common in Washington homes, and a wet crawlspace is often the first sign of a drainage or plumbing problem. Rusted buried steel gets replaced with polyethylene and tracer wire, and we use a listed transition riser where it rises to the house. Above-ground runs stay in steel. Everything is tested before the trench is backfilled, so a bad joint gets caught while it is still easy to reach.

    Propane Gas Line Service in Longview, WA

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. Our work covers propane yard lines, regulator replacement and fitting repairs, plus a check that every appliance has its propane orifices installed. Wrong orifices make a burner run dirty and unsafe. The test procedure is the same as for natural gas.

    Buried Yard Line Repair and Replacement in Longview, WA

    Many older homes have steel gas lines running from the meter to the house or out to a pool heater. The wrap fails eventually. Once the coating cracks, the pipe starts to corrode, and wet soil from sprinklers or a low spot in the yard speeds that up a great deal. Utilities get marked first. The new line is polyethylene with tracer wire, or coated steel where it has to be exposed, and we backfill and clean up the yard when the test passes.

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in Longview

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. Our plumbers total the BTU load, measure the run and size the pipe so the last appliance on the line still gets full pressure at peak use. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Gas Shut Off by the Utility in Longview

    Gas Leak Detection in Longview

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Longview

    Tightening or resealing a connection often runs $150 to $400. Bigger repairs cost more. After-hours emergencies or longer pipe replacements usually land between $500 and $2,500, depending on access, material and how much pipe is involved.

    A rotten egg smell is the big one, since utilities add a sulfur odorant to natural gas. Hissing is another sign. Other signs include a hissing sound near a pipe or appliance, dead plants over a buried line or a meter dial that moves with every appliance off, and any of them means leaving the house and calling the utility from outside.

    Many gas line repairs and all new gas line installations in Longview need a permit. You will know before we start. Our office pulls the permit, schedules the inspection and has a plumber on site to show the inspector the test gauge, which keeps your part of it to almost nothing.

    Every gas line repair we make includes a written warranty on parts and labor. Nothing goes back in service without a test.

    Simple repairs take an hour or two. Yard line replacements usually take a day, and permitted work adds inspection time.

    Our plumbers work with black iron, coated steel, copper where local code allows it, and CSST in homes across Washington. Each needs the right fittings.     We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Code decides where each one goes.
