    What Our Gas Leak Repair Visits Cover in Longview, WA

    Gas Leak Detection in Longview

    Some leaks announce themselves with a hiss you can hear across the room, and others lose a few cubic feet a day and only show up on the bill. Slow leaks are what the test gauge is for. We isolate, then sniff. We run the test twice, with appliance valves closed and then open, because some leaks only show downstream of a valve. Finding the exact joint keeps the job small.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Longview

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. Regulator vents and lockup pressure get checked along with every joint. Rooftop units leak too. Where downtime costs you money, we isolate only the affected branch and keep the rest of the building running. Staff get a short briefing on the shutoff locations.

    Permit and Inspection Coordination in Longview

    Replacing more than a connector usually means a permit, and in Longview the inspector will want to see a gauge holding pressure before the pipe is covered. We pull the permit and book the inspection ourselves. Most inspections take under twenty minutes. Test pressure and duration follow the local fuel gas code, which often means several psi for fifteen minutes or more. The signed permit card stays with your records.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Longview

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. We start with a gauge on the system to confirm there is a leak at all. Then the sniffer comes out. Our combustible gas detector reads in parts per million, so we can follow the reading fitting by fitting until it peaks. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final confirmation.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Longview

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. Any of those still in service should be replaced. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Longview, WA

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Connectors get bent every time a range is pulled out to clean behind it. Valves weep at the stem. We swap in a new coated stainless connector, add a quarter-turn ball valve and set a sediment trap where the code calls for it. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Interior Gas Pipe Repair in Longview, WA

    Inside the house, gas leaks hide at unions in the basement and at the threaded nipple behind the furnace, places nobody looks. We work from the meter inward, testing each section as we go. Drywall stays intact when possible. If the leak is inside a finished wall, running a new CSST line around it often costs less than opening and patching the wall. Nothing is turned back on until the gauge holds steady.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Longview

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Buried steel loses its coating to rocks in the backfill and then rusts through. Roots and settling do damage too. After an 811 locate, we excavate, cut out the failed run and lay polyethylene with a tracer wire beside it. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Longview trench rules.
