    Copper to PEX Conversions in Longview

    Copper lines can develop pinhole leaks from water chemistry, high velocity and sometimes soil contact under slabs. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is typically soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Once a home has had several pinhole leaks, more usually follow. Converting to PEX stops the cycle and lowers the risk of hidden water damage. We use approved transition fittings wherever new PEX connects to existing copper stubs or valves.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Longview

    We offer installation for both PEX-A and PEX-B piping and understand the distinct advantages of each. PEX-A, often preferred for its flexibility and ability to recover from minor kinks, uses expansion fittings. PEX-B is more rigid but also very durable, usually using crimp or compression fittings. Our experts in Longview can help you decide which type of PEX suits your project, considering factors like budget and application.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Longview

    The main water line brings water into your home, and a failing one can cause serious issues. We provide professional PEX main water line installation, replacing old, damaged lines with durable PEX. This upgrade improves water flow, reduces the risk of future leaks and offers superior resistance to ground shifting and corrosion. Our team ensures proper trenching and the connection to your municipal supply.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Longview, WA

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a tight seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can recover from kinks with a heat gun. It is our favored choice for many full repipes.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Longview, WA

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Many insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to sell and insure.
