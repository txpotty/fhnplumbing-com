    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Longview, WA

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Longview

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib. The relief valve lever gets lifted next. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Longview

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. We match the new valve to the heater's input rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Expansion Tank Service in Longview, WA

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.
