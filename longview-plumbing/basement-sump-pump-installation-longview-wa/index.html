    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Longview

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. The secondary, usually battery powered, sits beside it as a backup, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Two pumps, two check valves.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Tying a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is not allowed in most places, and it can draw a fine.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Both move water fine.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the check valve, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. The visit is short.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.
