    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Longview

    Testing in Longview, WA usually costs between $75 and $150 per assembly. That covers the visit, the test with a calibrated gauge and filing the report with your water provider. Several devices at one address usually get a lower per-unit price because the tester is already set up. Commercial lines cost more.

    Watts, Wilkins, Febco, Conbraco, Apollo and Zurn are all on our list. Our technicians carry kits for the popular models in each line, and less common parts can usually be ordered within a few days. Older models are usually fine.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco are the most common, and we also see Apollo and Zurn on commercial work. Common kits ride along.

    Installation and repair should be left to a licensed plumber who holds tester certification. Installing the wrong device, mounting a PVB too low or burying an RPZ in a pit can each fail inspection and leave the water unprotected. Providers can fine for it.

    Look for a steady leak from the device, a sudden drop in water pressure or discolored water at the tap. An RPZ that discharges from its relief port whenever pressure shifts usually has a leaking check. Get it tested soon.

    Testing must be done by a certified backflow tester using calibrated equipment. Providers reject anything else. The gauge also has to be calibrated within the past year, and that calibration date goes right on the report form.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A double check has the two checks but no relief valve, which limits it to lower hazards such as many fire and irrigation lines. Code decides which one.

    New installations and replacements often require a plumbing permit and an initial test. Each city and provider sets its own rules. We pull permits and handle the initial test, so the paperwork is done before the assembly goes into regular service.
