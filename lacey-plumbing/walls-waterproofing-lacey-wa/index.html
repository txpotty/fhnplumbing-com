    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Lacey, WA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Housing in Washington ranges from early 1900s homes to new construction, with plumbing materials that change a lot from one decade to the next. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Lacey, WA

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, six inches of drop over the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. Every option comes written out with its price.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Lacey, WA

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head spraying the wall instead of the plants, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Lacey

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Lacey take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days.     Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Lacey, WA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst.     Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day.     Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Lacey

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Crawlspaces and basements are both common in Washington homes, and a wet crawlspace is often the first sign of a drainage or plumbing problem. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Lacey

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to the method we use, the quality of the install and the soil conditions around your house. The sump pump will need replacing well before that. Most pumps last seven to ten years.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, with drain tile along the footing and a sump to pump it out.     Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. Drain tile can silt up over time.
