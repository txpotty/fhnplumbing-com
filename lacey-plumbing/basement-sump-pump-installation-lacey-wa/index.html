    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Lacey

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Lacey. A straight pump replacement usually does not, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. The paperwork is on us.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Both move water fine.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. New pits, new outlets and discharge routing are a different story, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in Lacey. Those parts call for a licensed plumber.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Tying a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is not allowed in most places, and it can draw a fine.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. You are dealing with wiring, pipe and concrete in one job, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. It takes a minute.
