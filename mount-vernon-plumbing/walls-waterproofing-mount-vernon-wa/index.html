    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Mount Vernon, WA

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Mount Vernon

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Washington has two very different climates, with long, wet and mild seasons in the west and hot, dry summers and cold winters in the east. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Mount Vernon

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Mount Vernon, WA

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. After that the wall gets cleaned, dried and coated with a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Mount Vernon, WA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Housing in Washington ranges from early 1900s homes to new construction, with plumbing materials that change a lot from one decade to the next. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Drainage System Integration in Mount Vernon, WA

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Footing drains, interior drain tile and French drains in the yard all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Mount Vernon, WA

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Mount Vernon, WA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Mount Vernon

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. A full exterior dig and membrane on a basement usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000, depending on depth, wall length and what sits against the house, while interior drain tile with a sump typically costs $4,000 to $12,000. Prices vary with access. We itemize every quote after the inspection.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, with drain tile along the footing and a sump to pump it out. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. Outside costs more.

    Sprinklers, nine times out of ten. Spray heads and drip lines aimed at the house soak the block every day, and poor grading or a downspout dumping at the corner adds more, so the masonry stays wet and leaves mineral deposits as it dries. Sometimes adjusting a single sprinkler head solves it.

    Some handy homeowners do. Rolling on a liquid membrane is not hard, but most leaks start at the seams, inside corners and drain, and a pan also needs a correct quarter-inch-per-foot slope plus a 24-hour flood test. A pro install lowers the odds of hidden damage.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but cannot hold back water under hydrostatic pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, and that is what a basement sitting in wet soil or a high water table calls for.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, so the studs and sheathing rot faster than if the wall had been left alone. We repair the leak and dry the cavity first.
