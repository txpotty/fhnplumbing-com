    Hydrojetting vs Snaking Cost Comparison in Mount Vernon, WA

    Snaking a main line typically costs $200 to $450, while residential hydrojetting usually runs $400 to $900 with a camera inspection. The jet is the bigger bill. Because jetting removes the buildup that causes repeat clogs, many homeowners spend less over a few years than they would on repeated snaking visits for the same line.

    Hydrojetting for Cast Iron Pipes in Mount Vernon, WA

    Older cast iron drains corrode and build scale on the inner wall, leaving a rough surface that grabs debris. Jetting can remove that buildup. Badly corroded cast iron with thin walls or cracks needs gentler settings or no jetting at all, and the camera tells us which before we start, along with whether the pipe is a candidate for lining.

    Same-Day Jetting for Backups in Mount Vernon

    Sewage in a tub or floor drain means the main is blocked. Stop using water first. Our technician opens the line, then jets it to remove the cause, and a camera pass after jetting shows whether the backup came from buildup alone or from a broken or root-filled section that needs repair.

    Preventive Hydrojet Maintenance in Mount Vernon

    Preventive jetting pays off for lines that clog more than once a year. Camera findings set the interval. A line with heavy grease might need jetting every six months, while a sewer with a few root entry points might only need it every 18 to 24 months, and the footage tells us which.

    Grease Trap Line Cleaning in Mount Vernon, WA

    Restaurant kitchen lines carry hot grease that hardens as it cools. By the time it reaches the interceptor, a lot of it has already coated the pipe. We jet the line from the kitchen to the interceptor in short passes, which keeps loosened grease from plugging the pipe farther down, and we coordinate with your pump-out company. Quarterly works for most.

    Water-Only Drain Cleaning in Mount Vernon, WA

    Hydrojetting uses only water, so nothing caustic goes into your pipes or septic tank. Chemical cleaners can damage pipe. Jetting removes the buildup physically, and the debris flushes to the sewer or septic tank as normal waste, so there is nothing left in the pipe to eat at joints or harm the bacteria a septic system depends on.
