    How We Install Showers in Mount Vernon, WA

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Mount Vernon, WA

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. We pull the tub, move the drain from the end of the tub to where the shower pan needs it and raise the valve to shower height, usually around 48 inches off the floor. On a slab, we cut concrete. The new shower is safer to step into.

    Steam Shower Plumbing in Mount Vernon

    Steam showers need four things: a generator, a water supply, a drain line and an electrical circuit. We install the generator within the maker's distance limits, plumb its supply and drain and run the steam line to the head inside the enclosure. An electrician runs the circuit. A sealed enclosure with a sloped ceiling keeps drips off you.

    Barrier-Free Walk-In Showers in Mount Vernon, WA

    Barrier-free entries need careful drain placement. We work with your tile setter on the waterproofing membrane and the slope, since water has to reach the drain before it can roll out the open side of the shower. East side homes near Mount Vernon and beyond face bigger temperature swings, which strain pipes, fittings and outdoor plumbing. Pan material follows from that.

    Budget Shower Upgrades in Mount Vernon, WA

    Budget showers can still be built right. A fiberglass or acrylic unit with a quality pressure-balance valve costs far less than a tiled shower, and when it is set on a mortar bed with a sealed drain, it lasts decades. The valve is where to spend. The quote covers everything up front.

    Tub and Shower Combo Installation in Mount Vernon

    Setting a tub is mostly about level and support. Acrylic and steel tubs get mortar or foam underneath, while cast iron rests on a level ledger, which keeps the caulk line intact. The valve goes in next. We set the spout and head heights to your layout and test the overflow gasket with water.

    Shower Stall Installation in Mount Vernon

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. We set fiberglass and acrylic bases in a bed of mortar or setting compound so the floor of the unit does not flex underfoot, since flex is what cracks a base within a couple of years. Then the drain goes in. Flow and temperature get tested after trim.

    Emergency Shower System Replacement in Mount Vernon, WA

    Water under a failed pan can rot a wood subfloor in a season. When a pan is cracked or a valve is leaking into the framing, we pull the damaged shower, check the joists and subfloor and install a new unit as quickly as the framing allows. The framing must be dry. Then the new shower goes in.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Mount Vernon

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. A wood floor gets dropped joists or a recessed pan, while a slab gets cut and lowered so the floor flows straight in. A linear drain often works best. Slope and waterproofing get planned before demo.
