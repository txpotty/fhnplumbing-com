    What Goes Into a Sewer Replacement in Mount Vernon, WA

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Mount Vernon, WA

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall flattens slowly under the dirt above it, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Sewer Line Repair vs. Full Replacement in Mount Vernon

    We look at the whole line before recommending either. A single bad joint can usually be dug and fixed. Breaks every few feet, roots at every joint, or a belly that holds water for twenty feet mean the rest of the pipe is close behind, and in that case a full replacement saves you from paying to dig the same yard twice.

    Traditional Trench Sewer Replacement in Mount Vernon

    An open trench gives full access to the pipe from the house to the main. We dig with a mini excavator and hand dig near utility marks. The old pipe comes out, the trench bottom gets a bed of gravel or sand, and new 4-inch PVC goes in at a steady quarter inch of fall per foot, confirmed with a level on every length. Then the camera.

    Sewer Pipe Material Upgrades in Mount Vernon, WA

    Plenty of older houses still drain through their first sewer pipe. Clay joints open up and let roots in, cast iron scales and cracks from the inside, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid 1900s, squashes into an oval over time. Replacement means PVC or HDPE.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement Options in Mount Vernon, WA

    Bursting and pulling replace the pipe. Lining patches the inside of the old one. We use bursting when the old line is broken or undersized, because it leaves you with brand new pipe rather than a liner inside an old one, and lining when the pipe is still round and mostly intact. Each has its place.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Mount Vernon, WA

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. A hairline crack is enough. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and cutting them out with a machine only buys a few months, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Mount Vernon

    Cast iron sewer pipe lasts a long time, though not indefinitely. Over decades it scales up inside and the bottom wears through. We replace it with PVC, and where cast iron remains under the house we use a shielded no-hub coupling at the transition. Clean seal.

    Under-Slab Sewer Replacement in Mount Vernon

    Sewer pipe under a slab is the hardest kind to reach. We have two ways in. We can cut and remove a strip of floor inside the house and patch it after, or tunnel under the slab from outside so the floors stay intact, and the right choice depends on the length of the run, the flooring, and what sits above the pipe.
