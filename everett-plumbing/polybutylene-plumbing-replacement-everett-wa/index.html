    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Everett

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. You may see it at the water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff. We inspect visible lines, check the stamps and trace runs through the attic. You get a clear answer and a written replacement estimate.

    Detecting Polybutylene Leaks in Everett

    Even small, hidden leaks from polybutylene pipes can cause significant damage to your Everett home's foundation or interior. Our technicians use modern leak detection equipment, including acoustic sensors and thermal imaging, to pinpoint the exact location of leaks without destructive guesswork.     Understanding Polybutylene Failure in Everett

    Polybutylene's built-in weakness stems from its chemical reaction with oxidants like chlorine, commonly used in municipal water treatment. Over time, this reaction causes the pipes and their plastic fittings to become brittle, leading to hairline cracks and eventual rupture. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is generally soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Hard water can also add stress to pipe that is already breaking down. Understanding this failure mechanism is important for diagnosing issues and recommending the most effective and permanent replacement strategy for your home.
