    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Everett, WA

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. We replace the pump and prime it properly. Then we watch the gauge through a full cycle.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Everett, WA

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. A pinhole in the suction pipe lets air in, the pump loses prime, and it runs dry until the seal burns up. The suction line gets a pressure test. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Everett

    Sand kills submersibles early. Grit in a well chews up impellers fast, so we look for it on the old pump and may set the new one higher or add a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.

    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Everett

    Pump and tank work as a pair. A tank that is too small or has lost its air charge makes the pump start dozens of extra times a day, and every start heats the motor. We check it on every pump job.

    Control Box and Pressure Switch Replacement in Everett, WA

    Not every dry faucet means a dead pump. A pressure switch with pitted contacts or a plugged sensing tube can stop the pump even though nothing is wrong down the well. The switch itself is a cheap part. We show you the burned contacts.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Everett

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Parts and control boxes are easy to find. We match the motor and the wire gauge to the depth of your well.

    Well Pump Sizing in Everett

    Sizing starts with the well log. We get depth and static level from the well log or a sounder, then a draw-down test shows what the well can really produce in gallons per minute. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Everett

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. On the way up, we look at the torque arrestor and the splices. Figure on one day.
