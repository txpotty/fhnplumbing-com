    Electric Water Heater Tune-Up in Bremerton

    Electric tanks fail at the lower element. Sediment buries the lower element until it overheats and fails, leaving you with a short supply of hot water. Both elements get tested. With power off, we check thermostats and connections.

    Tank Flush and Inspection in Bremerton

    Sediment forms from minerals in the water. We flush the tank, test the relief valve, check the anode and look over each fitting. Efficiency comes back. So does recovery time.

    Flushing a Tank Water Heater in Bremerton

    Popping and rumbling mean sediment. Calcium and magnesium settle on the bottom of the tank and form a layer that traps heat under it, so water boils in pockets beneath the scale and pops every time the burner or lower element fires. The tank drains until the water is clean. Heavy buildup may take several cycles.

    Water Heater Leak Checks in Bremerton, WA

    Small drips become floods. We inspect the supply connections, the relief valve outlet, the drain valve and the tank seams for moisture or rust trails, and we check the drain pan and the floor around the unit for signs of a slow leak. Rust stains are a warning. Early repairs protect floors and drywall.

    Gas Burner and Venting Checks in Bremerton

    A blue flame is what we want to see. Yellow or lazy flames mean poor combustion, so we clean the burner and combustion chamber, check the thermocouple or flame sensor, and confirm the vent has a proper draft and tight connections. Loose vents leak CO. The gas line and shutoff get leak tested.

    Anode Rod Checks in Bremerton

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. Once the rod is eaten down to its core wire, the water goes after the tank itself. We pull it and look. A new anode costs far less than a new tank.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Bremerton

    Some water eats anode rods fast. Depending on your water chemistry, a rod that should last five years might be spent in three, so we pull it on every visit instead of assuming it is fine because the tank is still young. We swap worn ones. A healthy rod keeps the tank from rusting through.
