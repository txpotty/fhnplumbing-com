    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Bremerton, WA

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in the water breaks it down from the inside. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some insurers will not write a policy on a house that still has it. Worth replacing.

    Trenchless Bores Under Driveways in Bremerton

    Directional boring drills a path under the obstacle and pulls the pipe back through it. It does not need the old pipe as a guide. We use it when the new line has to take a different route, say around a new addition or under a driveway poured after the house was built, and the launch and exit pits stay small. Heavy rock is the exception.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Bremerton

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. It costs more than PEX.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Bremerton

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Bremerton, WA

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is lighter and mostly goes indoors. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Bremerton

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so replacing the whole run is cheaper over five years. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Bremerton, WA

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Eastern and mountain areas of Washington freeze regularly and deeply, so insulated pipes and winterized irrigation systems are standard. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and keep it covered from sunlight at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.
