    How We Replace Polybutylene in Burien, WA

    PEX Pipe Installation in Burien, WA

    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Faster install means lower labor cost. Attic PEX stays out of UV, clear of flues and can lights and supported as the maker specifies, which protects the pipe and the warranty.

    Copper Replacement Option in Burien

    Type L copper appeals to some homeowners for its long history and rigid feel. Joints are soldered or pressed. Copper costs more than PEX and takes a little longer to install, so we help you weigh the cost, your water conditions and how long you plan to stay in the house.

    Partial Polybutylene Replacement in Burien, WA

    Full replacement is the right long-term fix, but it is not always possible right away. Budget and timing matter. We can replace the highest-risk sections first, like the lines and fittings near the water heater and in the attic, and plan the rest for later so you are not carrying all of the risk in the meantime. We will be honest about what is left.

    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Burien

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. The meter often tells first. Finding it lets us make a temporary repair and plan the full replacement.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Burien

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. House size, fixture count and how easy it is to reach the attic or crawlspace are the main factors, and we give you a detailed written quote after looking at the house in person.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. The build year matters. A house from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe and a past fitting leak should be replaced before the next failure.

    Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is generally soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Some insurers raise rates or decline coverage for homes with polybutylene.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Details are written up front. Certified PEX-A installs also carry the pipe manufacturer's long-term warranty, and we register it for you so the paperwork is on file if you ever need it.
