    Repiping Services We Handle in Burien, WA

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Burien, WA

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. Brittle pipe walls and cracked fittings mean one polybutylene leak usually signals risk across the whole house. Full replacement is the fix. Many insurers ask about it.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Burien

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. Our crews use Uponor or similar PEX-A, where the pipe end gets expanded and shrinks back over the fitting for a full-diameter connection. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Most homes are repiped with PEX-A in two to three days.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Burien, WA

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. Copper commonly costs 20 to 40 percent more than PEX, and some owners prefer it anyway for its long history. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Condo, Townhome and Multi-Family Repipes in Burien

    Shared walls and HOA rules make repiping attached homes more involved. We coordinate with the HOA, schedule water shutoffs that may affect neighbors and protect adjoining units from dust and noise as much as we can. Property managers get phased schedules. Big buildings get repiped by stack or wing, which lets residents stay home.

    Drywall Patching and Restoration in Burien, WA

    Repiping needs small openings in walls and ceilings to reach fixtures and pull pipe. We cut clean, square openings, protect the area around them and patch every hole once the plumbing passes its test. Textures get matched. We match knockdown, orange peel or smooth walls, so the patches are ready to prime and paint.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Burien

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Crawlspaces and basements are both common in Washington homes, and a wet crawlspace is often the first sign of a drainage or plumbing problem. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Burien, WA

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Low pressure, brown water and leaks at threaded joints are the typical signs. Old galvanized gets removed or abandoned and replaced with PEX or copper, and most homes see better pressure and clearer water right away.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Burien

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. That is often the first sign. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest. Old pipe stays in walls when pulling it would wreck finished surfaces.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Burien

    Most whole-home repipes in Burien, WA fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. The final number depends on square footage, how many fixtures need new lines, whether you choose PEX or copper and how much drywall has to be opened and patched along the route. We quote it in writing.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper is durable but costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX costs less, installs faster, handles freezing better and runs quietly, while copper tolerates sunlight and heat better and appeals to owners who want metal pipe, so the right pick depends on your house.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty, and PEX makers such as Uponor offer long-term warranties when certified installers use their system. You get the details before we start.

    Most whole-home repipes take three to five days, including patching. Large houses may need more time. We turn the water off in the morning and back on before we leave each evening, so you can cook, shower and do dishes at night even while the job is still in progress.

    Most families stay home the whole time. Water is off during work hours. Each evening the water comes back on, and we keep floors covered and clean up daily so the house stays livable.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It takes skill and time. A DIY repipe can leave hidden leaks inside walls, fail inspection or void the pipe manufacturer's warranty, and any of those can cost more than hiring a licensed crew in the first place.
