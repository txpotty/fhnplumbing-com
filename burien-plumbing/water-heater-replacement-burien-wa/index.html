    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in Burien

    The cost of water heater replacement in Burien, WA usually ranges from $1,200 to $2,500 for a standard tank unit, including installation. Tankless units can range from $3,000 to $5,000 or more, depending on the model and needed plumbing modifications. Factors like unit type, capacity, brand and labor complexity affect the final price.

    We install Bradford White, Rheem and A.O. Smith water heaters. Each offers reliable performance and a range of warranties.

    Most standard tank-style water heater replacements can be completed in 3 to 5 hours, assuming no major complications or plumbing reconfigurations. Tankless or hybrid installations might take a bit longer, often 5 to 8 hours, due to more complex venting or gas line changes.

    Most new water heaters come with a manufacturer's warranty ranging from 6 to 12 years on the tank and components, depending on the model and brand. FHN Plumbing also provides a labor warranty on our installation, so your investment is protected.

    While it might seem tempting, water heater replacement involves gas lines, high voltage electricity, water pressure and heavy equipment. It is a complex job best left to licensed professionals to ensure safety, proper installation and compliance with local plumbing codes, preventing potential leaks or hazards.

    Manufacturer tank warranties usually range from 6 to 12 years depending on the model. We also warranty our installation labor.

    A traditional water heater stores and heats a limited amount of water in a tank, whereas a tankless water heater heats water on demand as it flows through the unit. Tankless units offer endless hot water and can be more energy-efficient, but often have a higher upfront cost and may require gas line upgrades.

    Tank water heaters often last about 8 to 12 years, and hard water and sediment buildup can shorten that. Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Annual flushing and anode rod checks can help lengthen that life.
