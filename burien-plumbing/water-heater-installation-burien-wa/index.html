    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Burien

    A standard 40 to 50 gallon tank water heater usually costs $1,600 to $3,500 installed. Tankless installations often run $3,000 to $6,500 depending on gas line and venting needs.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by flow rate.

    An expansion tank absorbs excess water pressure created as water heats up inside the tank. Many homes in Burien, WA, particularly those with closed-loop plumbing systems, are required by code to have an expansion tank to protect the water heater and plumbing system from damage.

    To improve efficiency, make sure your new water heater is properly sized for your home, consider a tankless model for on-demand heating, insulate hot water pipes and set the thermostat to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scalding and reduce energy consumption.

    New water heaters usually come with a manufacturer's warranty on the tank and components, usually ranging from 6 to 12 years. FHN Plumbing also provides a labor warranty on our installation work, ensuring peace of mind. We will discuss exact warranty details for your chosen unit during the consultation.

    Most standard water heater installations, particularly for replacing a similar tank unit, can be completed within 3 to 4 hours. However, a conversion from a tank to a tankless system, or installations requiring major plumbing or electrical modifications, might take a full day or longer. We will provide an estimated timeline during our initial assessment.

    The correct size water heater depends on the number of people in your household and your peak hot water demand. For tank units, a 40 to 50 gallon tank is common for 2 to 4 people, while larger families might need 60 to 80 gallons. For tankless units, it is about flow rate (GPM) and temperature rise. Our plumbers will assess your needs to recommend the right size.

    The decision to repair or replace often depends on the age of your unit, the cost of the repair and its energy efficiency. If your water heater is over 10 years old, often breaks down, or the repair cost is more than 50% of a new unit, replacement is often the more cost-effective choice. Newer models also offer significantly better energy efficiency.
