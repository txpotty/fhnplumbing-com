    Propane Water Heater Repair in Tacoma

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Tacoma, WA

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. A good flame is blue and steady. Rust flakes on the burner send us to check the flue baffle next.

    Thermostat & Gas Valve Repair in Tacoma, WA

    Scalding water at the kitchen tap is a valve problem until proven otherwise. Some controls drift high as the sensor ages, and some dials get turned past hot. Our plumbers in Tacoma, WA measure the outlet temperature at the nearest fixture, compare it to the dial and replace the gas control if it is overshooting, because water at 140 degrees can burn a child's skin in a few seconds. Most homes do fine at 120.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Tacoma

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look.     Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Tacoma

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.
