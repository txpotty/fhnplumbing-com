    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Tacoma

    The cost of faucet repair in Tacoma can range from $120 to $350, depending on the type of faucet, the specific parts needed, and the difficulty of the repair. A simple washer replacement will be on the lower end, while a cartridge replacement in a high-end faucet might be more. We provide a written estimate before any work begins.

    If the faucet body is solid and parts are available, a repair is generally worth it. If it is corroded, cracked or discontinued, a new faucet is often the better choice.

    Generally, simple faucet repairs like replacing internal components, handles, or the entire faucet (if it's a like-for-like replacement) do not require a permit in Tacoma. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or significantly changing the fixture type, a permit might be necessary. Our plumbers know local codes and will let you know if a permit is required.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If a special part must be ordered, we may need a second short visit.

    Faucet repair involves fixing the existing fixture by replacing specific worn or broken internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Faucet replacement means removing the old faucet entirely and installing a brand new one. We recommend replacement when a faucet is severely damaged, very old, or if you simply want an upgrade.

    Most standard faucet repairs, such as replacing a washer or cartridge, can be completed by an skilled plumber in about 30 minutes to an hour. More complex issues or specialty faucets might take longer, but we strive for efficient service to minimize disruption to your home in Tacoma.

    Low water pressure can stem from several issues, including mineral deposits clogging the aerator or internal components, a worn-out cartridge, issues with your home's main water supply, or even problems with municipal water pressure. Our technicians perform a careful diagnosis to accurately pinpoint and resolve the cause.

    Low pressure at one faucet is usually a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure everywhere could mean a failing pressure regulator, a supply issue or old corroded pipes.
