    What Goes Into a PEX Job in Tacoma, WA

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Tacoma

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. After 40 or 50 years the bore is so choked with scale that a half-inch line delivers a trickle, and the water comes out tinted brown after it sits overnight. We replace every foot of it with PEX. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Tacoma, WA

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. With the studs bare, we can run PEX back to a manifold or to the nearest good trunk, set new stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a line for the fridge ice maker. An addition gets sized on its own. A new bath at the far end of the house may need a three-quarter inch feed to keep pressure up.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Tacoma

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool and a cold-expansion ring. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Tacoma

    The main from the meter to the house is the one line everything else depends on. A leaking old service line shows up as a soggy patch in the yard, a higher water bill and weak pressure everywhere. We trench and lay new PEX. At the house we add a new full-port ball valve for the main shutoff.

    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Tacoma

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, usually 18 to 36 inches down. Because PEX comes on a long coil, the whole run from the meter to the foundation can go in with zero buried fittings. Crawlspaces and basements are both common in Washington homes, and a wet crawlspace is often the first sign of a drainage or plumbing problem. It gets bedded in sand, not rocky backfill. Sharp rock against plastic pipe is how a new line fails early.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Tacoma

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Tacoma

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.
