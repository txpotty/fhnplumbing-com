    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Renton, WA

    On a double sink, the disposal goes on one bowl and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. The layout keeps both bowls draining and stops the disposal from pushing water into the second bowl, a problem when the tee is too close to the discharge. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in Renton

    Rough-in decides where the outlet and drain end up before the cabinets go in. For a kitchen with a disposal, we usually set the drain stub lower than a plain sink would need, so the trap can sit below the disposal outlet with room for proper fall. A switched outlet goes in at this stage. We mark its spot so it clears the trap.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Renton

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. We also check the circuit. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.

    Commercial Disposal Setup in Renton

    Restaurants and commercial kitchens in Renton need disposals built for constant use. These units usually require a dedicated circuit, a larger drain connection and a control panel that runs water automatically, and some sewer districts limit or ban them, so we check first. Downtime costs money.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in Renton

    For a common 1/2 or 3/4 HP disposal, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. We connect it to the drain, set the P-trap and wire it, then check the clearance under the sink, which can be tight in older Renton homes with shallow cabinets or a water filter mounted on the back wall. Cramped cabinets take some planning. A compact unit can solve it.

    Batch-Feed Disposal Installation in Renton, WA

    A batch-feed disposal will not start until the lid is in, and the lid holds a magnet that closes a switch inside the unit. No wall switch is needed, which makes them handy in kitchens where running a new switch would mean opening finished walls. You grind one load at a time. Families with small kids often like the trade.

    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Renton

    These disposals run only with the stopper lid seated, which adds safety for families with kids. Because they need no wall switch, they suit kitchens where a new switch would mean cutting drywall or a tile backsplash. Mounting and drains are standard.
