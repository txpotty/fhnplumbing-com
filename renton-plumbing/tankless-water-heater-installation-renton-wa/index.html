    What Goes Into a Tankless Conversion in Renton, WA

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Renton, WA

    Upfront, a tankless install in Renton, WA costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. We price both side by side.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Renton

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. A garage or outside wall near the gas meter can shorten the gas run and simplify venting, but it may lengthen the hot water run to the bathrooms. We weigh both. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Renton, WA

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. The vent changes as well. Both are on the estimate.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Renton, WA

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is generally soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. Flush it yearly. We install isolation valves for that.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Renton, WA

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. Their condensing and non-condensing lines both fit different homes, depending on the vent route and the budget you are working with. Non-condensing units vent in stainless. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Renton

    Commercial hot water loads in Renton run longer and harder than a house. A restaurant can run a dish machine, hand sinks and a mop sink together, and the rack must hold temperature through the rush. We rack units with a common manifold. One can be serviced while the rest run.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Renton

    Gas tankless units in Renton are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. Gas is the catch. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Renton

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and many models include a built-in recirculation pump. That pump can push hot water toward a far bathroom on a schedule, which shortens the wait at the tap without adding a separate pump under the sink. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. There is a condensate line to route as well.
