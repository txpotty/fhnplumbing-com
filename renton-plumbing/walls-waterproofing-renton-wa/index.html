    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Renton, WA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Housing in Washington ranges from early 1900s homes to new construction, with plumbing materials that change a lot from one decade to the next. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Renton, WA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer piped to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Renton

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Washington has two very different climates, with long, wet and mild seasons in the west and hot, dry summers and cold winters in the east. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Renton

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Glacial till and dense hardpan are common in western Washington, and they drain poorly enough to keep water against foundations and in crawlspaces. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Renton, WA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Washington has two very different climates, with long, wet and mild seasons in the west and hot, dry summers and cold winters in the east. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. Those weep holes empty into the interior drain if the house has one.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Renton

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Renton, WA

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Drainage System Integration in Renton, WA

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Footing drains, interior drain tile and French drains in the yard all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a gutter dumping at the corner. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.
