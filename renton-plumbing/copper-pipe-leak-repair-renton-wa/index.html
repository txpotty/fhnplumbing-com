    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Renton

    A simple solder repair usually runs $200 to $500. Access adds cost. A slab leak that needs specialized detection and repair could range from $800 to $3,000 or more, with reroutes at the top of that range. The written quote follows diagnosis.

    Start with water stains on walls and ceilings. Then check exposed pipe. Green or blue corrosion on fittings, musty odors, a higher bill and the sound of dripping inside walls all point to a copper leak, and low pressure at one fixture can mean a leak on that branch. A visit settles it.

    Wall thickness. K is thickest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, while Type M is lighter and more prone to pinholes over time. Our repairs use Type L.

    Water chemistry is a big factor. Soft water can be mildly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Pressure matters too. Water that is too acidic or too alkaline, aggressive soil around buried pipe, high pressure, poor installation and foundation movement each wear copper down or pull fittings apart. Most leaks have two or three causes.

    Wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors are the obvious sign. Pressure drops count too. The sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps, and green deposits on pipe you can see all suggest a leak. Check the meter with everything off.

    Shut off the main water valve. Then call (425) 491-3131. Turning off the main stops the damage while you wait, and opening a low faucet drains the pressure out of the line so the leak slows to a drip. Move valuables away from the water. We will walk you through it if needed.

    We typically use Type L copper. It is heavier than Type M. Type L lasts longer than the Type M found in many older homes, while press fittings or a PEX transition get used when a torch is a bad idea. Every part is lead-free.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. They will not last. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Treat it as a stopgap.
