    Most tub faucet replacements run $250 to $600 with a standard faucet. A trim and cartridge swap on a valve that stays in the wall sits near the low end, while replacing the valve itself, especially behind tile with no access panel, lands near the top. You get a written estimate first.

    Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Calcium and magnesium build up on cartridge seals and inside the spout diverter. It stiffens handles and wears seals early, leading to drips and a stuck diverter. Wiping the spout and trim often slows it.

    A worn cartridge or stem washer is the usual cause. On a single-handle valve the seals inside the cartridge have hardened or cracked, and on a two- or three-handle faucet the washer and the brass seat under it are worn, so water slips past even with the handle closed. Replacing the internals fixes it.
