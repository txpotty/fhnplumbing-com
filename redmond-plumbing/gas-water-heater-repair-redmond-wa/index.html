    What Our Gas Water Heater Repairs Cover in Redmond, WA

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Redmond

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Redmond, mostly in basements and utility closets. Parts for them are simpler, and a universal thermocouple fits most. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. One goes in while we are there.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Redmond

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water and years without a flush both make it worse. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Pilot Light Repair in Redmond

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. With a millivolt meter, it takes about a minute to see if it is putting out enough. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Redmond

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Redmond, WA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Redmond, WA. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. We keep both on the truck. Genuine parts keep the warranty intact.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Redmond, WA

    Restaurants and salons in Redmond, WA burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business. Commercial gas tanks often hold 75 to 100 gallons and fire at much higher BTU ratings. We work on those larger atmospheric and power vent units, including the electronic controls, the burner manifolds and the piping that ties two tanks together on one supply. Every hour down matters. We can often schedule the repair before opening or after close.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Redmond, WA

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. The burner comes out, the ports get brushed and the orifice gets cleared. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Redmond

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.
