    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Spokane

    Mid-century homes often have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, while some 1980s and 1990s homes were plumbed with gray polybutylene. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and add drain tile wherever water collects at the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Not every basement needs one.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Spokane

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Steep slopes and wet soils can move over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Spokane

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Spokane

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Spokane, WA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Constant moisture west of the mountains encourages mold, rot and corrosion around leaks, so small drips should not be ignored. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Spokane, WA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and makes sense when a patio or driveway sits against the foundation.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Spokane

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Constant moisture west of the mountains promotes mold, rot and corrosion around leaks, so small drips should not be ignored. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.
