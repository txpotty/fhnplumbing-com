    Sump Pump Installation Services in Seattle, WA

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Seattle

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. Because the motor is cooled by the surrounding water, they run quieter and can push more gallons per minute up a tall lift than most pedestal pumps. Lids close flat over them. We seal the cord grommet and the lid on every install.

    Primary Sump Pump Installation in Seattle

    A primary sump pump is the one doing the daily work, kicking on every time the float rises in the pit after rain or snowmelt. Sizing comes from two measurements we take on site, the pit diameter and the vertical lift to the discharge. Most homes need 1/3 or 1/2 HP. We usually pair that with a 1-1/2 inch discharge line.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Seattle

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi monitors go further and send a phone alert when the pump runs too long, the power drops or the water climbs past a set level. Landlords like them. They can go on a new pump or one that is already working.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Seattle, WA

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. The charger keeps the battery topped off. Batteries need replacing every few years. Finished basements get the most out of them.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Seattle

    No pit yet? We saw-cut the slab at the low corner, break out the concrete and dig down through soil, hardpan or rock to set an 18 by 22 inch basin. We backfill around the basin with washed gravel so groundwater has a path in. The slab gets patched with new concrete. The pump, lid and discharge go in last.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Seattle, WA

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. You can reach the motor and the switch without fishing anything out of the pit, which helps in a basement where the pit is shallow or tight. They are louder than submersibles. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Crawl Space Sump Pump Installation in Seattle, WA

    Crawl spaces with standing water breed mold and rot sill plates within a few seasons. A small sump basin at the low point fixes that. Clearance is usually under two feet. The discharge exits through the rim joist and drains away from the house.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Seattle, WA

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. We carry common pumps on the truck. Most swaps take about two hours. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Seattle

    Most Seattle, WA sump pump jobs land between $600 and $1,800. That range covers a quality pump, a new check valve, the discharge fittings and the labor to set and test it in a pit that already exists. Digging a pit adds cost. Every line item is on the quote.

    Manufacturer warranties usually run three to five years, longer on premium models. We also back our workmanship.

    Odd noises, rust, constant cycling or a pump that fails to start are the common signs. Any pump past seven years is worth a closer look, even if it seems fine, because the switch is usually the first part to go. An inspection settles it.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Cast iron pumps with mechanical switches tend to outlast plastic ones, and a pump that rests longer between cycles lasts longer. Yearly cleaning stretches it.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two or three hours. A new pit can take a full day.

    Three to five hours covers most standard jobs. Cutting a new pit or rerouting the electrical can turn it into an all-day job, especially when we hit rock or an old footing under the slab. You will know the timeline before we start.

    Most last seven to ten years. Idle pumps fail sooner, so pour a bucket in the pit every few months.

    A submersible sits in the pit under water, so it runs quieter and takes no floor space. The pedestal type mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes it easier to reach for service and usually cheaper to buy, though you will hear it. Either can do the job.
