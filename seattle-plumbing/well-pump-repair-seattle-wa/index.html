    Pressure Switch Replacement in Seattle

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, clogged sensing tubes and insect damage are common causes of failure. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank. This is one of the most budget-friendly well repairs.

    Well Pump Control Box & Electrical Repair in Seattle, WA

    Many well pump failures stem from electrical issues within the control box or wiring. This can include tripped breakers, faulty capacitors, or corroded connections. Our licensed technicians are trained to safely diagnose and repair these complex electrical components. We make sure all wiring meets Washington electrical codes, preventing hazards and ensuring reliable pump operation in Seattle homes.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Seattle, WA

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps prematurely. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank. Correct tank sizing protects the pump and gives steadier pressure at the tap.

    Jet Pump Repair in Seattle, WA

    Jet pumps, usually installed above ground, are often used for shallower wells or as booster pumps. Typical issues include loss of prime, seal leaks, or motor wear. We troubleshoot problems like air in the lines, clogged foot valves, or faulty pressure switches. Our team in Seattle is skilled at restoring prime and replacing worn components to get your jet pump operating smoothly again, a frequent need where water levels rise and fall.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Seattle, WA

    Variable speed systems like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by adjusting motor speed. When they fault out, the drive typically shows a code that guides diagnosis. We troubleshoot drives, sensors and wiring and repair or replace components. These systems need a smaller pressure tank but correct setup.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Repair in Seattle

    The cost of well pump repair in Seattle can vary a lot, typically ranging from $400 for minor electrical fixes to $2,500 or more for complex issues like replacing a submerged pump motor. Factors include the type of pump, the depth of the well, and the exact parts needed. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis.

    Well pump repair involves complex electrical components, high-pressure water systems, and often heavy equipment for pulling pumps from wells. DIY repair is usually not recommended due to safety risks and the potential for further damage. It's best left to experienced professionals to ensure a correct and safe repair.

    The length of a well pump repair depends on the issue. Simple fixes like a pressure switch replacement might take a few hours, while replacing a submersible pump can take a full day, especially if the well is deep. We strive for efficient service to restore your water as quickly as possible.

    Common causes include a waterlogged pressure tank, a clogged sediment filter, a failing pressure switch, a leak in the drop pipe or a falling water level. We test each to find the cause.

    Submersible pumps typically last 8 to 15 years, depending on run time, sand and water quality. Short cycling from a bad pressure tank can shorten that considerably.

    A constantly running well pump often indicates a leak in the system, a faulty pressure switch, or a waterlogged pressure tank. It could also mean the pump is undersized or struggling to keep up with demand. Our technicians in Seattle, WA can accurately diagnose the cause and recommend the appropriate repair.

    Well pump failures can be caused by various factors such as electrical problems (power surges, faulty wiring), mechanical wear and tear on pump components, sediment buildup in the well, or issues with the pressure tank or switch. Age and consistent use also play a role.

    Watch for sputtering faucets, low or fluctuating pressure, a pump that runs constantly, air in the lines or sudden spikes in your electric bill. A rapidly clicking pressure switch also signals trouble.
