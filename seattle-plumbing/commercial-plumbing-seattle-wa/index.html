    What Our Commercial Plumbers Do in Seattle, WA

    Commercial Pipe Repair and Repiping in Seattle, WA

    Older commercial buildings often have galvanized supply lines that are rusted nearly shut, or copper that has pinholed along hot water runs. Patching works for a while. When pinholes keep moving around the building, repiping the worst zones in Type L copper or PEX usually beats paying for more emergency visits. We map the system first. Work gets phased by floor or suite so tenants keep their water most of the day.

    Kitchen and Floor Drain Cleaning in Seattle

    Floor drains in commercial kitchens collect grease, rice, mop water and the odd utensil. Most kitchens see the same drain back up every few months. A sectional machine opens the line, and a jetter cleans grease off the walls of the 2-inch and 3-inch branches so it stays open longer. Trap primers get checked too.

    Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Seattle

    A burst supply line above a drop ceiling can soak inventory and ceiling tile in under an hour. Shut the main first. We can talk your staff through the shutoff while the plumber drives over, then isolate the broken branch and get the rest of the building back on water. Backups get handled the same way. We clear the main, camera it and tell you whether it will happen again.

    Commercial Drain Cleaning and Hydro-Jetting in Seattle

    Commercial drains carry grease, paper towels and food waste in volumes no house ever sees. A cable just pokes a hole. Our jetters push water at 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle that scours the full pipe wall, which clears 4-inch and 6-inch lines back to bare pipe. A jetted line stays open far longer than a cabled one. We see the most benefit in restaurants, schools and multi-family stacks.

    Backflow Prevention Assembly Service in Seattle

    A backflow assembly keeps water from your building, including irrigation lines, boiler water and chemical feeds, from flowing back into the public supply. Most providers want a yearly test. Our certified testers check reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valves and pressure vacuum breakers with calibrated differential gauges, then file the report with your water provider. Failed devices usually need a rebuild kit, not a new valve. We carry Watts, Wilkins and Febco kits for that reason.

    Tenant Improvement and Build-Out Plumbing in Seattle, WA

    New tenants usually need restrooms moved, a break room added or a kitchen built in a shell space. We work from stamped plans, coordinate rough-in with your general contractor and schedule inspections so the plumbing is never what holds up the opening date. Old lines get capped. Abandoned drains and supplies are cut back to the main and capped properly, which prevents dead legs and sewer gas later.

    Backflow Testing and Certification in Seattle

    Each backflow test takes about 30 minutes per assembly and requires a short water shutoff to that line. Plan for a brief outage. Our tester opens each test cock in order, records the check valve and relief valve readings on a calibrated gauge and compares them against the pass limits printed on the test form. We tag every passing device. The provider gets the report from us, and we keep a copy for your next test.

    Grease Trap and Interceptor Repair in Seattle, WA

    Food service sites need a grease trap or interceptor to keep fats, oils and grease out of the public sewer, and pretreatment inspectors check them. Odor is the first warning. We repair broken baffles, replace cracked lids and outlet tees, jet the inlet lines and confirm the unit is sized for your fixture count and flow rate. Slow kitchen sinks often mean the trap is full or the baffles are gone. Keeping the pumping log current helps at inspection time.
