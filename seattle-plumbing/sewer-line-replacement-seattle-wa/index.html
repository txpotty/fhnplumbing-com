    What Goes Into a Sewer Line Replacement in Seattle, WA

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement in Seattle, WA

    Two holes instead of one long trench. With bursting, a steel head splits the old pipe into the soil and pulls new HDPE into the same path. Lining is the other trenchless route. Lining puts a resin tube inside the old pipe and cures it hard, but only if the host pipe is still mostly round and has no major sags.

    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Seattle

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. A camera that dead-ends at broken clay or squashed fiber pipe means we have to dig, clean out the debris and replace that run. Being in the trench shows us what the surrounding soil is doing. Sometimes we find a washout or a root mat that explains why the pipe failed in the first place.

    Sewer Pipe Materials We Install in Seattle, WA

    PVC covers most of our trenched work. We lay SDR 35 PVC for most buried runs, Schedule 40 where a thicker wall is called for, and fused HDPE for pipe bursting. Steep slopes and wet soils can shift over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. Cast iron still gets used indoors on some jobs. The material choice depends on depth, soil and the method you pick.

    What Sewer Replacement Costs in Seattle, WA

    Four things set the price: length, depth, access and method. Trenching an open yard tends to be cheapest per foot, and bursting costs more per foot but avoids tearing out concrete. Driveways change the math fast. After the camera inspection you get a written price for each method that will work.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Seattle, WA

    Sometimes one joint is the whole problem. With only one bad section, we excavate a single small pit and splice in new PVC using shielded couplings. Far cheaper than a full run. You see the footage that supports the call.

    Sewer Codes and Inspections in Seattle, WA

    Inspectors check what gets buried. Washington adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code statewide with state amendments, and local building departments handle permits and inspections. On a sewer job that means pipe size, minimum fall, bedding material, cleanout locations and the connection at the main or the tap. We book the inspection and meet the inspector. Unpermitted pipe sometimes has to be uncovered again for inspection, at the owner's expense.

    Fixing a Bellied Sewer by Trench in Seattle, WA

    A belly is a low spot that holds water. Trenchless methods ride the old pipe, sag and all, which means a real belly has to be dug out and relaid on grade. Rocky ground slows the dig. We price that in after the locate shows depth. Each joint gets checked before the inspector sees it.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Seattle

    A winch pulls the bursting head. Because the head is a little wider than the old pipe, it cracks it into the surrounding dirt as the new HDPE follows. You get a pit at each end. With no joints in the new pipe, roots have no way back in.
