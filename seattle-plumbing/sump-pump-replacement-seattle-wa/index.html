    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Seattle

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle.     Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Seattle

    The cost of sump pump replacement in Seattle, WA typically ranges from $500 to $1,500, depending on the type of pump, submersible or pedestal, its horsepower and any necessary pit changes. Battery backup systems will add to this cost, usually between $300 and $800. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your needs.

    While it might seem straightforward, professional sump pump replacement is strongly recommended. It involves electrical connections, proper plumbing for the discharge line, and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding and comply with local Seattle codes.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Seattle homes benefit from a backup system due to storm risks.

    We install a variety of trusted sump pump brands known for their reliability and performance, including Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Our plumbers can help you pick the best brand and model for your home's requirements and budget.

    FHN Plumbing provides warranty coverage on both the sump pump unit itself, per the manufacturer's warranty, and our workmanship. We stand behind our installations for your peace of mind and a reliable, long-lasting solution.

    Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. Pumps that sit idle for long periods can fail earlier due to stuck switches.

    Simple replacements usually do not require permits. New discharge lines or electrical work may, depending on your city.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional's inspection.
