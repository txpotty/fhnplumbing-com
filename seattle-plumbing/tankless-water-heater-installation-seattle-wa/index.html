    Rinnai, Navien and Noritz Installation in Seattle, WA

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Seattle

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Seattle, WA

    Calcium scale is what kills most tankless units early. Soft water can be mildly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. A yearly flush prevents it. We install isolation valves for that.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Seattle, WA

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing units vent in PVC and drain condensate.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Seattle

    Propane homes in Seattle can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. Tank size matters too.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Seattle

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. Mounting near the gas meter on a garage or outside wall shortens the gas line and venting, though it can add distance to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. A far bath may need recirc.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Seattle, WA

    Upfront, a tankless install in Seattle, WA costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. Over the long haul, the math narrows. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. We price both side by side.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Seattle, WA

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Seattle, WA homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.
