    Emergency Sewer Replacement in Kirkland, WA

    Sewage coming up a floor drain is an emergency.     Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Kirkland

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. The bottom of a cast iron pipe goes first, and the camera will show a trench rusted through even when the top looks fine. Scale snags paper and grease. We swap the outdoor section for plastic pipe. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Kirkland

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. Backfill goes in compacted lifts so the trench line does not sink into a ditch a year later.

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Kirkland

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. After cutting roots and jetting the line, we pull in a resin-filled liner and inflate it until it cures against the old pipe wall. Collapsed pipe will not work. Lining shines under finished basements and slabs. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.

    Fixing a Bellied Sewer by Trench in Kirkland, WA

    A belly is a low spot that holds water. Trenchless methods ride the old pipe, sag and all, which means a real belly has to be dug out and relaid on grade. Rock adds time. We price that in after the locate shows depth. Joints are inspected by us before the city does.

    How We Price Sewer Replacement in Kirkland

    Length and depth drive the price. A forty-foot lateral four feet down in an open lawn costs a lot less than ninety feet under a driveway at eight feet, and we price both honestly. Method matters too. Your written estimate lists labor, pipe, equipment, permit fees and restoration as separate lines. No line items show up later.

    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Kirkland

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. A camera that dead-ends at broken clay or squashed fiber pipe means we have to dig, clean out the debris and replace that run. Digging also lets us see the soil around the break. Sometimes we find a washout or a root mat that explains why the pipe failed in the first place.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Kirkland, WA

If the camera shows a single offset or a cracked section and the rest of the line is round and clean, we dig one small hole at the marked depth and replace that piece with PVC and shielded couplings. That costs much less. We only suggest it when the footage backs it up.
