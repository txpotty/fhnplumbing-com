    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Kirkland

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. Watch the water meter.     Copper Pipe Replacement in Kirkland

    Copper is a solid alternative to PEX for poly replacement. It is rigid, handles heat well and has decades of history in homes. Plan on a higher price. We review your water and any history of copper pinholes nearby before recommending it over PEX.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Kirkland

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Municipal water west of the mountains in Washington is generally soft, while supplies on the drier east side tend to run harder. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Kirkland

    Most poly replacements in Kirkland fall between $4,000 and $15,000 or more. Size drives the price. Small homes with one bath and an open attic cost the least, and big multi-bath houses or copper jobs cost the most. The quote comes in writing at the visit.

    Some disruption is unavoidable, but most families stay home the whole time. Expect daytime shutoffs. Floors get covered, we clean daily and water comes back on each evening, so showers and dinner still happen at home.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. It is most common in homes from the late 1970s to mid 1990s.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Terms are on your estimate. Certified PEX-A installs also carry the pipe manufacturer's long-term warranty, and we register it for you so the paperwork is on file if you ever need it.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Certain insurers surcharge or refuse homes with poly.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Since poly can look normal right before it splits, a home from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe should plan a replacement even before any leaks.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. Copper jobs use domestic Type L, and we keep one PEX brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.
