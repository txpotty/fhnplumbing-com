    Repiping Services We Handle in Kirkland, WA

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Kirkland

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Kirkland

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. Clogged aerators are a clue. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest. Old pipe stays in walls when pulling it would wreck finished surfaces.

    Urgent Repipes After Repeated Failures in Kirkland, WA

    A widespread pipe failure sometimes forces a repipe sooner than you planned. We make the house safe and usable first, with temporary repairs that get you water, then schedule the full repipe as soon as the crew and permit allow. We move fast. Even urgent repipes usually take just two to four days after we begin.

    Commercial Repipe Services in Kirkland

    Older commercial buildings in Kirkland often have galvanized risers, copper that has pinholed or plastic pipe that no longer meets code. We replace supply risers, branch lines and drains while keeping offices, shops or apartments running as much as possible. Shutoffs get scheduled ahead.     Slab Leak Reroutes in Kirkland

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. We can reroute a single hot or cold branch overhead and abandon the leaking pipe in the slab, which costs far less than a repipe and avoids breaking the floor. This works well for a first leak. If the house has had several leaks, we will walk through why a full repipe usually costs less over time.

    Copper Repiping in Kirkland, WA

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Expect a slightly longer job. Copper repipes usually cost more and take a day longer than PEX, mostly because every turn needs a fitting.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Kirkland, WA

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. Brittle pipe walls and cracked fittings mean one polybutylene leak usually signals risk across the whole house. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Kirkland

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. Our estimator counts fixtures, checks access, finds the main and asks about any planned remodels before choosing a route. Then we size the lines. Bigger homes may need a 1-inch trunk so the shower does not drop when the dishwasher fills.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Kirkland

    PEX repipes in Kirkland commonly run $4,000 to $15,000. Copper costs more. Expect copper to land around $8,000 to $25,000 or more, with house size, bathroom count and attic or crawlspace access driving most of the price. We give you an itemized price after the walk-through.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. Viega and Zurn PEX show up on some jobs, but we keep one brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Swapping out polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized makes a home easier to insure and sell, especially with a permit record and transferable warranty.

    Most repipes in Kirkland, WA take three to five working days for an average house. Big houses take more time. Copper adds time because each turn needs a fitting, and patching usually takes one more day after the plumbing passes.

    Soft water can be slightly corrosive to copper over time, which sometimes shows up as pinhole leaks in older supply lines. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Flow drops as the bore narrows. Scale also speeds corrosion in metal lines, which is why PEX-A plus a softener or filter added during a repipe can protect the new system and your fixtures.

    Repeated slab leaks, pinhole leaks, low pressure, discolored water or polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized pipe are all signs. Repeat repairs say the most.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Openings are usually small. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It is not a weekend job. Hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided pipe warranty from a DIY job can easily cost more than a licensed crew would have.
