    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Kirkland, WA

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Kirkland

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. Sump pumps push groundwater out to the yard, ejectors push sewage to the sewer, and only the ejector pit must be sealed and vented against gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Kirkland

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. A full-port valve gets added.

    Grinder Pump Installation in Kirkland

    Grinder pumps chop waste into slurry before pumping it, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. A grinder makes sense when the sewer is distant, uphill or pressurized, such as a pressure sewer where each home pumps into a shared main. They cost more. A standard ejector often covers a basement bath.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Kirkland, WA

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Emergency Ejector Pump Replacement in Kirkland, WA

    A dead ejector pump means the basement fixtures stop working until it is fixed. Stop using those fixtures, including the washer if it drains to the pit, then call (425) 491-3131 and we aim to have a plumber and pump at your door the same day. Replacements are often quick.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Kirkland, WA

    Businesses in Kirkland, WA with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Downtime costs money. Each pump can carry the load alone.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Kirkland

    An ejector failure usually announces itself with the alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Stop all use of the basement fixtures and call, because once a plumber is on site most emergency replacements take just a few hours. Trucks stock popular models.

    Laundry and Wet Bar Ejector Pumps in Kirkland, WA

    Below-grade laundry rooms and wet bars produce gray water that still needs to be lifted to the sewer. A compact gray water pump handles it, sized for the 15 or more gallons a washer drops at once, with a lint filter to keep fibers off the impeller. Filters save impellers.
