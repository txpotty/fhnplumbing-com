    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Marysville

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Marysville's older homes take the most damage. They also put our techs at risk. Standing water full of caustic chemicals splashes when we open the trap, so tell us if you poured anything in before we arrived.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. Once the tech sees the cleanout and asks which fixtures are slow, they can give you a closer estimate of how long it will run.

    Routine drain cleaning in Marysville, WA does not usually need a permit. Major repair, sewer replacement or excavation in the yard generally does. We handle that paperwork. If the rooter work turns up a problem that needs a permit, we tell you before any repair gets scheduled.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera helps set the schedule. Seeing how fast roots came back since the last cutting tells us whether to plan a yearly visit or stretch it to two years.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Jetting scours the whole pipe with high-pressure water and strips the grease, scale and sludge a cable slides past. Repeat clogs usually need jetting.
