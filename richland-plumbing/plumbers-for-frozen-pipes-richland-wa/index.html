    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Richland

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Richland usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Prevention costs less. Insulation, heat cable and frost-free faucets usually cost less than one burst repair.

    Ice can split a pipe while frozen, but the leak only appears once the ice melts and water flows again. The crack was there all along. We test every thawed line, and you should watch nearby ceilings for a day or two.

    Check which fixtures work. The frozen section sits between the last working fixture and the first dead one. A dead sink on an outside wall usually means ice in that wall, which a thermal camera can confirm.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. If the frozen section is inside a wall, a ceiling or under the floor, call a plumber instead, because you cannot see whether it has already split, and thawing it can release water into the structure.

    Disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and backflow preventers, and let a faucet drip on very cold nights. Open sink cabinets on outside walls. If you leave town during cold weather, keep the heat at 55 degrees or higher and consider shutting off the main and draining the lines, which is the most reliable way to avoid coming home to a flooded house.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Parts carry the maker's warranty. It covers the repair, not a future freeze, so we recommend protection for exposed lines.
