    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Richland, WA

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. The float rod gets checked for bends, and the stops get checked for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Richland, WA

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Crawlspaces and deep window wells often have small pumps that clear runoff after a storm. We clean their screens, test the floats and follow the discharge until it reaches daylight or an approved drain, because a line that dead-ends under the house just moves the water around. Disconnecting hoses, insulating crawlspace pipes and covering hose bibs before the first freeze prevents most winter damage.

    Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Richland, WA

    Some Wayne models draw water through a top suction intake, which helps them avoid pulling in the sludge that settles at the bottom of a pit. We check that intake and the float mechanism for buildup. Any brand can rattle its discharge fittings loose over time. Easy fix.

    Float Switch Inspection in Richland, WA

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. We watch the float through a full cycle. Then we set the on and off points so the pump runs longer, less often.

    Check Valve and Discharge Pipe Repair in Richland, WA

    A bad check valve can nearly double how often the pump starts. Every startup puts heat into the motor. We swap worn valves for quiet, spring-loaded models and inspect the discharge pipe for cracks and loose couplings all the way to the exit point. The water should exit well away from the slab or foundation so it does not soak back toward the pit.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Richland, WA

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. A tethered float can tangle on the pump body or catch on the side of the basin. Vertical floats hang up once minerals coat the rod. In tight pits where nothing has room to swing, we can switch you to an electronic sensor switch with no moving float at all.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Richland, WA

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Submersible Sump Pump Service in Richland

    Submersible pumps sit at the bottom of the pit, which keeps them quiet but hides the problems. We lift the unit out to look at the seals and the cord. A cracked cord usually means a new pump. We also clean the intake screen and check the housing for corrosion, which shows up fast on pumps that sit in standing water for years.

    Neighborhood Sump Pump Maintenance Near Me in Richland, WA

    Water pools in predictable places around a house: the downspout corners, the low side of the yard and wherever the grade slopes back toward the wall. We look at all of that while we are outside checking the discharge. A little regrading can cut the pump's workload.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without one, the pump ends up moving the same water again and again. That short cycling wears out the motor early and runs up the electric bill.
