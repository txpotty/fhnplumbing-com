    Softener Systems We Replace in Richland, WA

    TAC Salt-Free Conditioners in Richland

    Template-assisted crystallization, or TAC, is the salt-free media with real independent test data behind it, and it is the only kind we install. The tank goes on the main line downstream of the filter, with no power or drain needed. We do not sell clamp-on magnet devices.

    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Richland, WA

    Resin beads break down after 10 to 15 years, faster on chlorinated city water, and the softener keeps regenerating while the water slowly turns hard again. Rebedding with new resin can be cheaper than a new system. The valve's age decides it. An old valve on new resin is a short-lived fix.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Replacement in Richland

    Salt-based softeners pull calcium and magnesium out of the water by trading them for sodium on a bed of resin beads, which is the only method that actually makes water soft. We replace timer units with metered valves that regenerate on gallons used. You will buy less salt. We set the brine refill to your hardness so the system is not dumping salt down the drain every few days.

    Commercial Water Softener Replacement in Richland

    Restaurants and laundromats run softeners hard, and a commercial unit often needs a 1.5-inch or 2-inch valve and twin tanks to keep up with flow. Peak flow drives the sizing. We work around your business hours.

    Long-Term Softener Costs in Richland, WA

    Long-term cost on a softener is mostly salt and water. Compared with a timer unit, a metered valve set efficiently often uses a third less salt, which matters over a fifteen-year life. We compare brands on that basis.

    Softener Loop Installation in Richland, WA

    Softener loops are the pair of stubbed-out lines near the garage wall or water heater where a softener ties into the main. Homes without one get a loop cut in, with shutoffs and a bypass. Hose bibs stay off it. We run the drain to a standpipe or floor drain through an air gap.

    Salt-Based Softener Swaps in Richland, WA

    Swapping a salt-based softener in Washington homes means disconnecting the old resin and brine tanks, cutting in new fittings if the loop is corroded, and setting the new valve. We pressurize and check every joint. We set an air gap on the drain line.

    Replacing Fleck Water Softeners in Richland, WA

    Fleck control valves, the 5600SXT and 9100SXT in particular, are on a large share of the softeners we see in Washington homes. If the valve is old enough that parts are hard to find, a new valve on a new tank is the smarter buy. Current Fleck valves fit standard tanks.
