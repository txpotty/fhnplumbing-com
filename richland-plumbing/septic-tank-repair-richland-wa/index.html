    Septic Tee Repair in Richland

    Sanitary tees on the inlet and outlet can crack or get knocked off by the hose on a pump truck. Without the outlet tee, the floating scum layer drifts into the laterals and plugs the gravel. We glue in a new Schedule 40 tee. An effluent filter goes on if the outlet has room.

    Septic Line Clog Clearing in Richland

    A slow toilet and a gurgle in the tub usually point to a clog between the house and the tank. We run a camera down the line first, then clear it with a jetter sized for 4-inch sewer pipe. Grease and wipes cause most of these, and the fix after clearing depends on which one we pull out. Roots mean a joint has opened.

    Commercial Septic Repair in Richland, WA

    Churches and small offices on septic run more water through the tank than any house, and a commercial kitchen adds grease on top of that.     Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Richland, WA

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. With no outlet baffle, sludge flows right out to the leach lines. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Drain Field Line Jetting in Richland

    Distribution laterals clog from the inside with solids and slime, especially after years without pumping. A jetter goes into each lateral from the D-box or an end cleanout, and the water that comes back tells us how bad it is. Roots get cut. If the soil itself has sealed, jetting will not fix it, and we say so.

    Septic Tank Leak Repair in Richland

    A tank that never fills to the outlet, or one that fills slowly with groundwater after rain, has a leak. We pump it down and look at the walls and seams with a light, and dye flushed down a fixture shows us where the water moves. Hairline cracks get a hydraulic cement patch. Split tanks get replaced.

    House-to-Tank Line Repair in Richland

    Gurgling drains right after a pump-out point to the line between the house and the tank, not the tank itself. That section sags or breaks from roots and settling soil. We camera it from the cleanout and mark the bad spot on the surface. Then we dig one hole, not a trench. A second cleanout near the tank goes in while it is open.
