    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Richland, WA

    Water lines tend to break on a Saturday night. Ours is a 24 hour line.     PEX Water Line Repair in Richland, WA

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Richland, WA

    Besides repairs, FHN Plumbing handles service line upgrades when a commercial tenant needs more flow, like a restaurant adding a dish line or a second restroom. Bigger meters need bigger pipe. We size it to the fixture load. Then we coordinate with the water provider on the tap and meter change, set the backflow assembly and get the inspection signed off, so the tenant is not held up waiting on paperwork.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Richland

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Richland

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Richland, WA

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. Shutting the water off comes first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, setting up a temporary supply to the house if the job will take a while. Same-day repairs are the norm. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Richland, WA

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    Main Water Line Repair in Richland

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Richland

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Full replacement costs more. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can top $5,000 when the run is long, deep or under hardscape, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    Age is the biggest cause. Galvanized rusts and copper pits. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. Polybutylene too, when we find it. For new work in Richland, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Most jurisdictions in Washington require a permit for a full service line replacement. Spot repairs vary. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, and an inspector looks at the new pipe, the bedding and the burial depth before backfill. FHN Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, with the coverage listed on your quote before we begin. Keep the invoice. It is your proof of coverage.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, including the locate and backfill. Full replacements are a day. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Small repairs often do not. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.
