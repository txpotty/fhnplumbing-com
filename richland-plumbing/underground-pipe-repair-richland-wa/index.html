    Simple buried pipe repairs usually run $500 to $2,500. Big jobs climb higher. Extensive trenchless sewer replacements can be $5,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on where the pipe runs, how bad the damage is and the method used to fix it. We quote after inspection.

    For water lines we use PEX or Type K copper. Both are rated for burial. Sewer and drain work uses Schedule 40 PVC, and trenchless jobs use fused HDPE, and every one of these holds up to soil and water far better than galvanized or clay. Local code decides the final pick.

    Many utilities offer a one-time leak adjustment. Ask them about it. Most want proof that the leak was repaired promptly, and we give you an invoice and repair documentation that shows the date, location and fix. Rules differ by provider.

    Less digging is the big one. The yard stays mostly intact. Trenchless methods leave driveways, patios and established landscaping alone, and the savings on putting the yard back often cover the higher method price. Jobs usually finish sooner.

    Wet spots or extra-green grass are the classic signs. So is a jump in the bill. A sudden drop in pressure, a sewage or sulfur smell in the yard, or gurgling drains may mean a broken pipe underground on the supply or drain side. Check the meter with everything off.

    Check the meter with every fixture off. Look at the leak indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere, with soggy ground or a hiss near the meter often pointing to the main line. We can confirm it fast.

    Our labor warranty is written into the quote. Terms vary by repair. A spot repair and a full service replacement carry different coverage because the scope, materials and risks are different from one job to the next. Pipe makers add their own coverage.
