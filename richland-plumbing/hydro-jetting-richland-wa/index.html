    Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking Cost Comparison in Richland, WA

    While snaking might be cheaper upfront for simple clogs, hydro jetting offers a more thorough and long-lasting solution, often saving money in the long run for Richland, WA residents. Snaking usually punches a hole through a blockage, leaving residue behind, which can lead to recurring clogs. Hydro jetting cleans the entire pipe, preventing future issues and reducing the frequency of service calls, making it a more economical choice for persistent drain problems.

    High-Pressure Water Jetting for Drain Cleaning in Richland

    High-pressure water jetting, or hydro jetting, is a non-invasive method that uses highly pressurized water to clear and clean pipes. Our equipment in Richland can generate water pressures up to 4,000 PSI, powerful enough to break apart stubborn blockages and scour pipe walls clean without chemicals. This method is effective for various pipe materials, from PVC to cast iron, and is an environmentally friendly choice for deep drain cleaning.

    Kitchen Line Grease Jetting in Richland, WA

    Kitchen drains are the most common grease trouble spot in any home or restaurant. Grease clings to pipe walls and hardens, especially where the line runs flat under a slab. Jetting melts and scrubs this buildup away instead of just poking a hole through it. We use smaller hose and nozzles sized for 1-1/2 and 2 inch kitchen lines. This keeps your sink and dishwasher draining properly for much longer.

    Preventive Maintenance Jetting in Richland

    Scale Removal in Cast Iron Pipes in Richland

    Cast iron drains in older homes build up rust tubercles and mineral scale, and hard water speeds that process. Jetting with the right nozzle can strip much of this buildup and restore flow. We keep pressure in a safe range for aged iron and watch the camera for thin spots. This cleaning is also the standard prep step before pipe lining. Clean pipe walls help a liner bond properly.

    Root Cutting With Hydro Jetting in Richland

    Specialty root-cutting nozzles spin or use focused streams to slice through fine roots inside a sewer. This is effective on root hairs that a cable saw can leave behind. Roots from yard trees and shrubs often enter at clay pipe joints or cracks. Jetting clears them out and gives a clean view of each entry point on camera. That helps you decide whether lining or a spot repair is the right move.
