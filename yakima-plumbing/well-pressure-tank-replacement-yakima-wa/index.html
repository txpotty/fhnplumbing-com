    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Yakima

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and confirm the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Yakima

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and choke flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water.     Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Yakima

    The cost for well pressure tank replacement in Yakima, WA usually ranges from $700 to $1,800, depending on the tank size, brand, and complexity of the installation. This includes the tank itself, labor, and any necessary fittings. We provide a detailed quote after assessing your specific system needs.

    If your switch is more than a few years old or the contacts are pitted, replacing it with the tank is low-cost insurance. It also lets us match the switch settings to the new tank.

    A like-for-like tank replacement often does not require a permit in most counties. Electrical changes or new piping may, and we check local rules for you.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, fully isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are often preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    Most pressure tanks last 10 to 15 years. Hard water, sand and frequent cycling can shorten that, especially when the pre-charge is never checked.

    Rapid pump cycling, pressure that swings up and down, and water coming out of the air valve are common signs. A tank that sounds solid and full when tapped near the top is usually waterlogged.

    Many residential tanks carry 5 to 7 year manufacturer warranties. We add our own labor warranty on the install.

    Common signs include your well pump cycling on and off very often (short-cycling), fluctuating water pressure at your faucets, no water coming from the tap, or a waterlogged tank when you tap on it. Visible rust or leaks around the tank are also clear indicators.
