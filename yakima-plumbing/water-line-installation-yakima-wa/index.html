    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Installation in Yakima

    Residential water lines usually run $1,800 to $5,500 depending on length, soil and material. Rock, boring or copper can raise the total to $6,000 to $10,000 or more.

    Steep slopes and wet soils can shift over time, which stresses buried sewer and water lines and sometimes separates joints. Soil that shifts and settles puts steady stress on buried pipe. Flexible pipe like PEX is popular for exactly that reason, and why sand bedding and careful backfill matter as much as the pipe itself when the ground around it keeps moving. Sand cushions the line.

    Some homeowners dig their own trench to save money. Connecting to the meter and passing inspection are better left to a licensed plumber, since a bad joint underground can leak for months before anyone notices.

    In most communities the homeowner owns the line from the meter to the house, and the utility owns the meter and the street side. Your water provider confirms the split before we start.

    Uponor Wirsbo and Viega PureFlow cover most of our PEX work. Inside the house we run Type L copper, and buried runs get Type K, sourced from manufacturers that meet ASTM standards, so the pipe holds up in the conditions it will actually see. Wall thickness matters in the ground.

    This is a job for a licensed plumber. It involves excavation, sizing, connections and code rules, plus coordination with the water utility at the meter, and a poor install can lead to leaks, water damage and contamination of your drinking water. The risk is not worth it.

    Our water line installs carry a warranty on parts and labor. You see the warranty terms on your estimate, and the pipe manufacturer adds its own long coverage, which on PEX and HDPE often runs decades when the pipe is installed to spec. We keep the install records.

    PEX and copper are the usual picks, with HDPE on long runs.
